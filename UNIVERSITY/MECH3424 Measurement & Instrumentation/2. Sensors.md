# Strain Gauges - Piezoresistivity
- Metal or semiconductor resistor elongated or compressed, changes conductivity = Piezoresistive effect
	- Strain = displacement, acceleration, pressure, temperature, liquid level, stress, force, torque
$$\frac{\delta R}{R}=S\epsilon|S\approx 2\to 4(metals), S\approx 40\to 200 (semiconductors)$$
- 3 Strain gauges required to completely measure strain in a plane if principle axes are not know (Rosette strain gauge)
- 2 if principal axis known and aligned at 45$\degree$ 
![[Pasted image 20260728121241.png]]
- $TCE$ is the coefficient of thermal expansion, metal strain gauge and structure should have the same TCE. Semiconductor are HUGELY temperature sensitive, hence where a metal Strain Gauge is better

# Specifying Pressure Sensors
### Gage Pressure (psig)
- Relative to ambient atmospheric pressure (760 torr) - one side must be vented to the local environment
![[Pasted image 20260728121618.png]]
### Differential Pressure (psid)
- Measured relative to a  specific reference pressure
- If the pressure is $1atm$, the differential pressure is equal to gage pressure
- $output=P_2 - P_1$ 
![[Pasted image 20260728121854.png]]
### Absolute Pressure (psia) 
- Includes atmospheric pressure and is measured relative to vacuum $0psi$ 
- Reference side is isolated from the local environment
- $output = psig + ambientAtmPressure$

### Hot Wire Anemometer
- An anemometer is an instrument for measuring (wind) speed
- In steady state, heat loss from liquid flow and heat generation are balanced $$i^{2}R=c(a+\sqrt{v} (T_{wire}- T_{ambient}))$$
- Advantages: excellent spatial resolution, High frequency response
- Issues:  Fragile, can be used only in clean gas flows, needs to be re calibrated often due to dust accumulation, high cost
# Temperature Sensors
- **Contact**
	- Sensors that measure their own temperature, assuming no heat flow between the object touching it and the sensor (thermal equilibrium)
	- This requires the sensor volume is small compared to the objects volume and the objects temperature is not rapidly changing
	- Thermocouples, thermistors, RTDs
- **Non-contact**
	- Requires a measurement of and understanding of spectral emissivity
	- Useful for fragile or wet surfaces
	- Substances that are chemically reactive or electrically noisy
	- Advantageous in measuring temperature of very large surfaces, which would otherwise require a large array
	- IR sensor
### Thermocouples
- Thermocouples incorporate dissimilar metal wires joined at least at one end that generate a voltage difference (∆V) which varies with temperature gradient (∆T) - based on the Seebeck coefficient (S) of a metal junction. They have small and non-linear output voltages. Used for especially high temperature applications -above a few hundred degrees. Require a known reference temperature junction to yield  absolute readings. Feature: zero-current flow = no self heating
$$T_{tip}=T_{ref}+\frac{V_{out}}{(S_{A}-S_{B)}}$$
- Sensitivity $\approx 10mV/\degree C$ 
- *Extremely* high temperatures
![[Pasted image 20260728142400.png|495]]
### Thermistor
- Thermistors are semiconductor materials (metal oxides-chromium, cobalt, copper, iron, manganese, nickel) with a high temperature sensitive electrical resistance. More precise and faster response than an RTD but far more non-linear.
- Features:
	- Accuracy to $\pm 0.1\degree C - \pm 0.2\degree C$
	- Typically specified at $25\degree C$
	- Limited range nominally of $-40\degree C - 150\degree C$
	- Stable - not affected by aging
	- Resistance - from $2k\Omega - 100k\Omega$ at $25\degree C$
- Very non-linear, makes circuit design difficult, can linearise by using matching thermistor pairs, log-amplifier circuits or look up tables
$$R=R_{0}e^{\beta(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{T_{0}} )}$$
![[Pasted image 20260728142644.png]]
### Resistance Temperature Detectors (RTDs)
- Same principle as thermistors
- Materials are metal, with a positive temperature coefficient
- Range wider than thermistors $-200\degree C < T< 800\degree C$
- Accuracy $\approx \pm 0.1\degree C$
- Linear approximation $R=R_{0}(1+\alpha T)$
### Semiconductor Temperature Sensors
- Based on fundamental physics of transistors/diodes
- One of the most common, again based on silicon band gap
- Five broad categories:

- Advantages: mass produced (low cost), linear outputs, relatively small size, good accuracy if calibrated, suited for embedded applications
- Issues: limited temperature range ($-50 - 150\degree C$), semiconductor temperature sensors packages are not well designed thermally leading to thermal delay, output signal may oscillate unless precautions are taken, not electrically or mechanically robust
### Liquid in Glass Thermometers
- Fluid-expansion devices, typified by the household thermometer, generally come in two main classifications: the mercury type and the organic-liquid (alcohol) type. Versions employing gas instead of liquid are also available. Mercury is considered an environmental hazard, so there are regulations governing the shipment of devices that contain it.
- Advantages: no power or wiring leads, no explosion hazards, long term stable even after repeated cycling
- Issues: data not easily recorded or transmitted, cannot make spot or point measurements, easy to break, low reading accuracy, mercury a hazard
### Labels, Crayons and Paints, Tabs
- Irreversible phase change at a specific temperature
- Advantages: very low thermal mass, visual indication quick, easy to apply, temperature range $ambient \to 1500\degree C$, nominal accuracy $\pm 1\degree C$, allows surfaces to be mapped by applying to various locations
- Issues: non-reversible, mixing of melting type indicators affects their operation, colour change and melting point can be missed, only indicate peak temperature was met

# Emissivity 
- Emissive $\epsilon$ defines the efficiency which an object emits or absorbs radiation
- $\epsilon = 1$ is a blackbody, a perfect emitter (absorber)
- $\epsilon < 1$ is a greybody
- Stefan-Boltzmann Law $$P=\epsilon \sigma AT^{4}$$
- Wien's displacement law $$\lambda_{max}T=c_{1}| c_{1}=2.898\times10^{6}mmK$$
![[Pasted image 20260728130502.png]]
### Pyroelectric Sensor
- Pyroelectric materials are characterized by having spontaneous electric polarization, which is altered by temperature changes as infrared light illuminates the elements. Pyroelectric detectors convert infrared light to electrical signals.
- Advantages: Very low bandwidth $5Hz$, elements (pixels) = 1,2x1 or 2x2, bandwidth with inbuilt filter $5-14\micro m(IR)$, inexpensive, noise equivalent power (NEP) $=1.1\times 10^{-9}W/Hz^{0.5}$, responsivity = $3.7\times 10^{5}V/W$, incident power (MAX) = $0.2W$

# Optical Sensors
- The Light Dependent Resistor (LDR) (CdS). Many names including the photoresistor, photoconductor, photoconductive cell, or photocell (∆λ=450-800nm) Very Inexpensive. 
- Material from which it’s made determines the wavelength detection window: 
	- Silicon – Visible & Near infrared (IR) (∆λ=300-1000nm)  
	- Lead sulfide- and indium antimonide- mid-IR (∆λ=2-8μm)  
	- Germanium-copper - far-IR (∆λ=8-20μm)
### Photodiodes
- Silicon 
	- inexpensive  
	- Fast : 20 ns switching  
	- Operates from VIS (400-700 nm) to near IR (does not detect heat sources)  
	- Responsivity:= 0.62 A/W @ λ=850 nm  
- Features:   
- Operate typically with zero-volt applied across terminals  
- Produces an output current ∝ detected Power (requires a transimpedance amplifier to produce voltage output)  
- Robust  
- High power  
- Far more sensitive than pyroelectrics thermopile –  
- But much lower wavelength and far smaller wavelength range (∆λ) of operation ~ x2 more sensitive than a photoconductor (LDR)

# CCD Arrays vs CMOS Arrays
- CCD = charge coupled devices
	- Single wire, shifts data out serially
- CMOS = complimentary metal-oxide semiconductor
	- Random RC addressing (amp-per-signal)
- Uses: 1D array for scanners, copiers; 2D array for cameras
![[Pasted image 20260728131339.png]]
### Imager Sizes
- Imaging features:
	- Operate at 15 or 30 fps
	- Much higher frame rates if required
	- CCD shift register can clock up to $20MHz$
	- Good for low light levels (shift slower)
	- High light level saturate pixels - blooming
- Debugging tool
	- These imagers are great optical testing tools
