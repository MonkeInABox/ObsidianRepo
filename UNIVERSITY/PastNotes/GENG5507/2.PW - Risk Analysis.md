# What is Risk
- Defined in AS/NZS ISO 31000:2018 as "the effect of uncertainty on objectives"
	- An effect is a deviation from the expected. It can be positive, negative or both and can create or result in opportunities and threats
	- Objectives can have different aspects and categories, and can be applied at different levels
	- Risk is usually expressed in terms of the risk sources, potential events, and their consequences and their likelihood
![[Pasted image 20260304100127.png]]
- Risk Management: coordinated activities to direct and control an organisation with regard to risk
- Risk Source: element which alone or in combination has the ability to give rise to risk
- Event: occurrence or change of a particular set of circumstances
	- An event can have one or more occurrences, and can have several causes and several consequences
	- An event can also be something that is expected which does not happen or something that is not expected that does
- Consequence: outcome of event affecting adjectives
- Likelihood: chance of something happening
- Control: measure that maintains and/or modifies risk
- Stakeholder: person or organisation that can affect, be affected by or perceive themselves to be affected by a decision or activity

# ISO 31000
- ISO = International Standards Organisation
- ISO 31000 can be:
	- Used by any public, private or community enterprise, association, group or individual
	- Applied throughout the life of an organisation, and to a wide range of activities, including strategies and decisions, operations, processes, functions, projects, services and assets
	- Applied to any type of risk, whatever its nature, whether having positive or negative consequences
- Scope, context and criteria:
	- Every org and situation is different so should be fit for purpose and appropriately scoped, with clear objectives, context, factors and criteria to evaluate significance of risk.
- Risk Assessment: overall identification, systematic, iterative, collaborative
- Risk Identification:
	- Find, recognise and describe risks
	- Factors:
		- Tangible and intangible sources
		- causes
		- Events
		- Threats, vulnerabilities
		- Emerging risks
		- Context
		- Nature and value of assets and resources
		- Time
		- Biases, assumptions and beliefs
		- Reliability of information and limitations of knowledge
- Risk Analysis
	- Factors:
		- Likelihood of events and consequences
		- Nature and magnitude of consequences
		- Complexity and connectivity
		- Time
		- Effectiveness of existing controls
		- Sensitivity and confidence levels
	- May be influenced by opinions, bias, perception of risks and judgements, quality of information, limitations of techniques
		- Should be considered, documented and communicated to decision makers
- Risk Evaluation: to support decisions
	- Comparing the results of the RA with the established risk criteria to determine where action is required
	- Decisions:
		- Nothing further
		- Risk treatment options
		- Undertake further analysis
		- Maintain existing controls
		- Reconsider objectives
	- Take into account the wider context and consequences
	- Should be recorded, communicated and validated at appropriate levels
- Risk Treatment; select and implement options
	- Iterative process of:
		- Formulating and selecting risk treatment options
		- Planning and implementing
		- Determining whether residual risk is acceptable
		- Considering further treatment
	- Wider context and consequences
	- Recorded, communicated and validated
![[Pasted image 20260304102504.png]]
- Risk Treatment Plans: specify how the treatment options will be implemented
	- Information includes:
		- Rationale and benefits
		- Accountability and responsible parties
		- Proposed actions
		- Resources required
		- Performance measures
		- Constraints
		- Reporting and monitoring
		- Actions to be undertaken and completed

# Risk Analysis
- The choice of analysis method is influenced by:
	- Context, objectives, available resources
- Three levels:
	- Strategic (management systems), project, equipment
- Approaches:
	- Qualitative
	- Semi-quantitative (semi-QRA)
	- Quantitative (QRA)
	- None are more superior than the other
- Qualitative:
	- Descriptions
	- Used when:
		- QRA not required
		- To perform initial screening
		- Level of risk does not justify the time or resources needed
		- Numerical data not available or inadequate
- Semi-QRA:
	- Numbers to provide a relative measure
	- Should not be used to give an appearance of a level of precision which does not exist
	- Measurement scales need not be linear
	- Text often used as a descriptor
- QRA:
	- Calculated level of risk
	- The context and assumptions that underlie the quantitative techniques should be clearly stated and understood
	- Can be plotted on a risk diagram (usually a log scale) with estimates of uncertainty
- Generic steps:
	1. Select hazardous sequence or scenario
	2. Estimate likelihood, from historical data
	3. Estimate consequence, health, economic, environmental or safety impact
	4. Total expected risk value expressed as $R=\sum _{i}F_{i}C_{i}$ 
	5. Risk matrices must be used to visualise results
	6. Estimation of uncertainty

# Hazards and Controls
- Hazards are a risk source
	- Hazards are often classified into groups
	- Can be identified on a day-to-day basis
- Controls are measures that maintain or modify risk
	- Policies, devices, practices or other conditions
![[Pasted image 20260304105522.png]]
- Barriers are a type of risk control
	- Separate people from the hazard
	- Can be tangible physical barriers or distance, etc.
- Natural hazards are an important type to consider
	- Earthquake standards etc
- Challenges
	- Once controls are identified the requirements for maintenance must also be identified
	- Barriers can fail
		- Physical processes
		- Malfunction
		- Human practices and decisions
		- Poor design/maintenance

# FMEA
- Failure Mode Effects Analysis
- A systemised group of activities intended to:
	- Recognise and evaluate potential failure of a product/process and its effect
	- Identify actions which could eliminate or reduce the chance of a potential failure occurring
	- Document the process
- The FMEA process focuses on what we want the equipment to do, not what it actually is
- By identifying functions, we can identify situations when the equipment does not perform the required function and focus on related causes and effects
- Done at new design, process or tech
	- During life cycle as well
	- FMEA can be applied to products and processes
![[Pasted image 20260304110402.png]]
- What is needed:
	- An appropriate team 
	- A table for assessing consequence (severity)
	- A table for assessing frequency of occurrence (frequency)
		- Usually with a rating, a description, frequency, probability
	- A table for assessing detection effectiveness
	- Lots of coffee and good humour
- Define system, subsystem, components and their functions
- Use a risk matrix, severity often across X and frequency across Y
- RPN: Risk Priority Number
	- As $R=S\times P$ for risk
	- $RPN = S \times P \times D = S \times O \times D$
		- S = Severity/Consequence
		- P = Probability
		- D = Number indicating change of IDing and eliminating
		- O sometimes used instead of P
