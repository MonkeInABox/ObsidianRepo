- A safety case is a document produced by the operator of a facility which:
	- Identifies the hazards and risks
	- Describes how the risks are controlled
	- Describes the safety management system in place to ensure the controls are effectively and consistently applied
# Safety Case vs Prescriptive Regimes
- Countries have legislation and regulations that govern health and safety in general and practice in specific industries
- The role of the regulator is to administer the provisions of their legislation. They may have wide-ranging powers for enforcement and compliance
- Broadly speaking there are two schools of thought about safety regulation. One is a prescriptive approach and the other is a performance-based or goal-setting approach
- Prescriptive Approach:
	- Product: 
		- Specific design features are required
		- Assurances is provided by inspection by licensed practitioners
		- Products comply with standards and may be certified against these
	- Process:
		- The process and/or standards specify the process to be used rather than a specific feature of the product or system
		- Assurance is based on if the process was followed and sometimes on the quality of the process
- Performance-based or Goal-setting Approach:
	- Approach sets a goal and is usually up to the applicant to decide how to meet the goal
	- The goal may be a risk target
	- It focuses on desired, measurable outcomes rather than required product features or prescriptive processes
	- The certification authority or regulator specifies a threshold of acceptable performance and a means of assuring the threshold has been met
	- The development of a Safety Case is an integral part of this approach
- Common principles underpinning safety cases
	- Those who create risks are responsible for controlling those risks
	- Safe operations are achieved by setting and achieving goals rather than by following prescriptive rules
	- All risks must be reduced such that they are below a specified threshold of acceptability
- Must's in safety case development
	- Must be produced by the operator of an installation
	- Must identify the safety critical aspects of the facility, both technical and managerial
	- Must be defined appropriate performance standards for the operation of the safety critical aspects
	- Workforce must be involved
## The role of the regulator in a safety case regime
- The safety case is produced in the knowledge that it will be scrutinised by a component and independent regulator
- The regulator assesses safety cases and accepts a safety case if it is satisfies that the arrangements proposed demonstrate that the risks will be reduced to as low as is reasonably practicable (ALARP)
- The regulator conducts inspections to monitor the operator's application of the safety cases in practice
- In the case of offshore oil and gas, the regulator is NOPSEMA 
## Challenges for safety cases
- A safety analysis is performed on an engineered and often social system where there is no complete mathematical theory to base arguments and guarantee completeness
- Confirmation bias is a tendency for people to favour information that confirms their preconceptions or hypotheses regardless of whether the information is true. If the goal is to prove the system is safe, they will focus on the evidence that shows it is safe and create an argument for safety
- The analysis is often limited to what is likely or expected, not what could be catastrophic. Simply arguing that the most likely case will be safe is not adequate: Most accidents involve unlikely events, often because of wrong assumptions about what is likely to happen and about how the system will operate or be operated in practice
