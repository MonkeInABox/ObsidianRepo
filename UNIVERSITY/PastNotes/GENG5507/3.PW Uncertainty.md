## Societal Risk
- The risk of defined consequences related to the population that may be affected by hazardous events in a certain region. Refers to the realisation of a risk where many are likely to be negatively affected
- Calculated as the number of events/time x consequences/event
- **Individual Risk**: concerns the annual probability of a negative event on a single individual. Calculated as societal risk/population at risk
- Examples of sites that could present significant societal risk include:
	- Chemical plants where liquefied toxic substances are manufactured, stored or used
	- Some water treatment plants where liquefied toxic chemicals are stored for use in water purification
	- Large liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and liquefied natural gas (LNG) storage facilities

## F-N Curves for Representing Societal Risks
- An F-N curve can be used to show historical data for different hazards at a societal level
- Display the probability of having N or more fatalities per year, as a function of N on a double logarithmic scale.
- The y axis is the fatality frequency at which N or more people are affected
- For a number of fatalities N from event i, the frequency $F_{N}=\Sigma_{i}F_{i}$
- $F_{N}$ is the cumulative frequency of all events $i$ where the number of fatalities is greater than or equal to $N$

## Expectation Value (EV) or Potential Loss of Life (PLL)
- EV is the average number of persons receiving the specified level of harm per year
- For fatalities this is called PLL which is the measure of how many fatalities per year are implied in the risk assessment
- The EV or PLL is simply the sum of the products of all the f-N pairs i.e. $PLL = \Sigma fN[people/year]$
- The EV or PLL, sometimes also called the average Rate of Death (ROD), is useful in Cost Benefit Analysis and ALARP demonstrations as it can be multiplied by the Value of Statistical life to determine the value of the benefit that could be gained by reducing the risk. $EV = \Sigma fN$
- Whilst the EV is reported with units "expected casualties per year", the value in real cases is typically very small

## Reporting Actual Data 
- Fatal Accident Rate (FAR) is defined as the number of fatalities per 100 million (exposed) hours
$$FAR = \frac{No.FatalitiesOverMyears\times 10^{8}}{TotalNo.HoursWorkedinMYears}$$
- Safe work Australia defines the fatality rate as fatalities per 100,000 workers

# Quantitative Risk Assessment
- QRA is a systematic, model based procedure for investigating ways in which complex systems operate
- Model how human, hardware and software elements of a system interact
- Used to assess the most significant contributors to risk of the system
## 3 Basic Questions
1. What can go wrong that could lead to exposure of hazards (associated with specific scenarios)?
2. How likely is this to happen?
3. If it happens, what consequences are expected?
## Establishing Consequences for Realisation of Uncontrolled Hazards or Failure of Engineering Controls
- Consequence estimates are often complex and require an estimation of who and what is impacted and the nature of the impact
- For example with a toxic release this depends on the particular material, the dispersion mechanism, the number of people/flora/fauna/soil affected and the nature of how they are affected
- In most industries it is a requirements to use structured and documented methods to identify scenarios, hazards, likelihood and consequence estimates and controls
- Quantitative Risk Assessment (QRA) is used in high hazard industries which use models and numbers to quantify the likelihood and/or consequence of events

## Value of a QRA
- The outputs of the QRA are used to:
	- Highlight system design and operational deficiencies
	- Prioritise resources to improve design and operation
	- Identify elements of the system that contribute substantially to the risks
	- Examine the uncertainty associated with these estimates
	- Test the effectiveness of the various risk-reduction strategies available
	- QRA is widely used in the development of safety cases. They are a regulatory requirement in high hazard industries
- Strengths
	- A systematic examination of the design and operation of a complex
	- Consideration of influence of system interactions and human-system interfaces
	- Consideration of assumptions and uncertainties
- However:
	- Errors result from weak models and lack of data
	- Problematic for estimating low frequency (but high impact events)
	- Challenge with modelling complex dependent systems and extreme operating conditions
## Expressing Uncertainty
- All the calculations involve uncertainties, approximations and assumptions
- Good practice is to make these explicit to decision makers

## Safety Cases
- QRA is widely used in the development of Safety Cases
- Safety Cases are a regulatory requirement in high hazard industries
- A safety case for a facility includes a:
	- Description of the facility
	- Formal safety assessment
	- Safety management system

## Regulators
- Regulatory powers are the powers used by government agencies to ensure individuals and industry comply with legislative requirements, and to respond to instances of non-compliance
