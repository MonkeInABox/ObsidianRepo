# What is Maintenance
- The combination of all technical, administrative and managerial actions during the life cycle of an item intended to retain it in, or restore it to, a state in which it can perform the required function
- Required function: what do we need the asset to do in a given context
	- **CONTEXT IS KING** - guy that is talking
	- Has to maintain a *design score* when measuring performance vs. time
	- When has an asset fail? There is a **NEED** in a certain context, once performance has fallen under there, then it has failed
	- Retain is preventative, before it is under the need
	- Restore is corrective, after it is under the need
	- After confidence intervals, we get uncertainty, the need is function is **objective**

- In a car:
	- Corrective maintenance: puncture repair
		- You have to be prepared for these things
	- Inspection: inspecting the wear on the tires, oil check, listening to engine start up
	- Condition monitoring: compression test on cylinders
	- Opportunistic: air filters, wheel alignment
	- Scheduled replacement: drive chains, spark plugs
	- Scheduled overhaul: engine rebuild
- Categories of failure consequences: hidden or evident, safety impact, environmental impact, operational impact
- Hidden failures: 
	- These failures where the loss of function, under normal circumstances, would not be detected
	- Normally applies to protective devices which are not fail safe
	- Smoke detector, etc
	- High pressure alarm 
	- PM tasks are appropriate if they reduce the risks associated with failure of these protective devices to tolerable levels
- Safety and Environmental Consequences/Impact:
	- A failure has safety consequences if this failure creates a risk to personnel safety
	- A failure has environmental consequences if this failure creates a risk of environmental damage
	- PM tasks to address failures with safety or environmental consequences are appropriate if they reduce the risk to tolerable levels
- Operational Consequences:
	- Include: 
		- Lost production, loss of quality, increased operating costs, loss of customer service
	- They are financial
	- These can all be quantified in financial terms, so PM tasks are appropriate if in the long run they are more cost effective than allowing the equipment to fail

	- Primary - why it exists
	- Secondary - **E**(environment)**S**(safety)**C**(comfort, control, containment)**A**(appearance)**P**(protective)**E**(efficiency)

> [!NOTE] Functional failures 
> Does not \[verb] \[object] at \[performance standard]

# Maintenance Strategies
- Condition monitoring tasks are applicable when:
	- There is a clearly defined potential failure condition
	- The P-F interval can be reasonably defined
	- The task can be done at a frequency which permits effective action to avoid the consequence of failure
	- The frequency of a condition monitoring task is determined by the P-F interval
- The P is the point at which a potential detection technique can detect deterioration
- There is traditional fixed interval task selection
	- When the conditional probability of failure starts to rapidly increase after a specified age
	- Most items will survive to that age
	- We can state with some certainty what that age is
- Run to failure task selection (run to maximum life)
	- Without safety or environmental consequences
	- When cheaper, in the long run, to allow the item to fail, than it is to prevent it from failing
- Failure finding task selection
	- Hidden failure 
	- Possible without destroying it
	- Possible to test
	- Performing the test does not significantly increase the risk of inducing the multiple failure we are seeking to avoid

# Maleny Water Pumping Station
The Maleny ClearWater Pumping station was built in 1975. The five Clearwater pumps transfer clean water from the station to a reservoir (and two backwash pumps). Normally there are 3‐4 pumps operating in parallel. The function of the pump station is “to move water from the clearwater tank to the reservoir at a rate of 20‐54 Ml/day depending on demand”.
![[Pasted image 20260326135640.png]]
- The maintenance strategy for the station is as follows:
	- Pumps are replaced based on their age
	- Condition monitoring using vibration analysis is conducted monthly on the motor and pump bearings
	- Once a year the electrical connections are supposed to be checked with thermographic methods to identify hot spots and loose connections however records about these inspections were not available
## Investigation; analysis conducted in 2004 at the pump station
- Planned and unplanned work:
	- In an operating environment, asset managers seek to manage equipment to avoid unexpected failures or unplanned work. Unplanned work is generally classified as work that is not part of the weekly maintenance plan (planning window); failures occur within the planning window and need to be dealt with immediately in order to restore capacity.
![[Pasted image 20260326140255.png]]
- Sources of failures:
	- In order to investigate the source of these costs unplanned work orders were broken down into mechanical and electrical failures. 90% of all failures were found to be electrical, the 10% of mechanical failures were due to broken flow switches, pipe work and valve leaks. A **Pareto** chart (Figure 3) shows the source or cause of failures events or sources.
![[Pasted image 20260326140405.png]]
- Reset failures are associated with electrical problems when the pump has either failed to start or failed in operation. They are generally associated with poor troubleshooting skills. Contactors failures are associated with wear on the contactors in electrical starter system or problems with the PLC control logic. Protection failures are associates with the motor protection and soft start system. Citect are associated with the PLC due to logic errors, control system crashes and communications issues. Flow failures are associated with the flow instrumentation; these are often associated with the physical parts of the measurement system such as the paddle. Thermal failures are associated with the thermistor in the motor when it detects high temperature.

### Main causes of failure
- Electrical and PLC failures causing reset

## What was the outcome of the electrical upgrade on reliability
The MTBF was significantly improved in the electrical systems
- MTTF is 5 times higher after the upgrade for reset and protection, 9 times for Citect
- Beta is less so more infant mortality
