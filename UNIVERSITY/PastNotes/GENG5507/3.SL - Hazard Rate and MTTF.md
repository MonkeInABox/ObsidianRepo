# Reliability Function $R(x)$
- Probability of survival to at least time $x$
- $R(x)=P\{X\geq x\} = \int^{\infty}_{x}f_{X}(t)dt$ 
- $R(x) = 1 - F(x)$, in case of continuous, not necessarily when discrete

# Failure Rate (Hazard Rate) Function
- Instantaneous failure rate $h(t)$
- $h(t) \times \delta(t)$ is approximately the conditional probability that an item fails during an interval $\delta t$, given it has survived to time $t$ 
$$h(t) = \frac{f(t)}{1-F(t)}=\frac{f(t)}{R(t)}$$
- Suppose an item has not failed up to time $t$, what is the probability that it fails in time interval $[t, t+\delta t]$?
	- $A = \{itemSurvivedUpTo(t)\}$
	- $B=\{itemFailsIn[t,\delta t]\}$
	- This a conditional probability: $P(B|A)=\frac{P(B\cap A)}{P(A)}$ 
- Dividing both sides by $\delta t$, we obtain the average rate of failure in $[t, t+\delta t]$, referred to as failure rate or hazard rate, and as $\delta t \rightarrow 0$  
$h(t)=\frac{F'(t)}{R(t)}=\frac{f(t)}{R(t)}=\frac{f(t)}{1-F(t)}$

# Mean Time To Failure
- The mean (expected value) of time to failure T
- Used for non-repairable items
- Gives a measure of central location/tendency
- Calculation of MTTF: for a time to failure T with density f(t) and reliability R(t)
$$MTTF = \int^{\infty}_{0}tf(t)dt = \int^{\infty}_{0}R(t)dt$$
![[Pasted image 20260317083436.png]]
## MTTF for Different Distributions
- Exponential time to failure with parameter $\lambda$ $$MTTF=\int^{\infty}_{0}R(t)dt=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\lambda t}dt=\frac{1}{\lambda}$$
- Weibull time to failure with parameters $\beta$ and $\eta$ $$MTTF = \int ^{\infty}_{0}R(t)dt = \int^{\infty}_{0}e^{\frac{-t}{\eta}^{\beta}}dt=\eta \Gamma (1+\frac{1}{\beta})$$
- Median time to failure is such that $$P(T\geq t_{med})=0.5$$
- The most likely observed failure time is defined as $$f(t_{mode})=max_{0\leq t \leq \infty}f(t)$$
- Variance of time to failure $$\sigma^{2}=\int^{\infty}_{0}t^{2}f(t)dt - MTTF^{2}$$

# Hazard Rate for Normal Distribution
$$f(t)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{\frac{-(t-\mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}$$
- Standard Normal Distribution $\mu$ = 0, $\sigma$ = 1
$$f(t) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^\frac{-t^{2}}{2}$$
$$\Phi(z)=P\{Z\leq z\}=\int^{z}_{-\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^\frac{-t^{2}}{2}dt$$

# Lognormal Distribution: Characteristics
$$f(t)=\frac{1}{\sigma t\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{(lnt-\mu)^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}$$
$$X=ln(T)\sim N(\mu, \sigma^{2})$$
- $\mu$ and $\sigma$ are of $X$
![[Pasted image 20260317084857.png]]
- Advantageous for:
	- Having a close relationship with normal distribution
	- Often better fit to reliability data such as population with wear-out characteristics
	- Being suitable for modelling processes that degrade over time
	- Hazard rate is not monotone
