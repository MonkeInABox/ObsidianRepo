# Driving Robots
- Drive systems:
	- Single wheel
	- Differential
	- Tracked
	- Synchro
	- Ackermann
	- Omni-Directional
		- Front ones are inwards
- Can be non-holonomic or holonomic (holonomic means no restrictions on its DOF)
- A single driven wheel is like that thing in Cars that has a wheel at the front that moves it with two wheels at the back
	- Can turn on the spot
	- Only two motors required
	- Decoupling of drive and turn
	- Driving straight is simple
	- Complex mechanics for turning driven wheel
- Differential drive
	- Two wheels with motors, driven independently
	- Both same speed for straight driving
	- Only two motors required
	- Simple mechanics (fixed shaft)
	- Driving straight requires complex control software
	- Eyebots are differential drives with 2 DC motors with encapsulated encoders
- Skid steering (tracked)
	- Similar to differential drive
	- 2 motors, one for each track
	- Essentially two pairs of motored wheels
	- Can go over rougher terrain
	- Positioning is inaccurate (slip)
	- Additional sensors required
- Synchro Drive
	- 2 motors, one for driving all three wheels, one for turning all three
	- Robot can drive in all directions
	- Robot cannot turn on the spot
	- Often a circular shape
	- Driving straight is easy
	- Complex mechanical structure
- Ackermann
	- Only two motors
	- Relatively simple
	- No turning on the spot
	- Rear driven wheels will slip in curve unless differential
