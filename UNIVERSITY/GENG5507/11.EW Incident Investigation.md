# Case Study Part 1
Work in the resources industry often involves large heavy equipment that must be removed from  active service for repair or renewal. Examples of these units include valves, pumps, conveyor pulleys,  crusher mantles, or haul truck tyres. Typically they can range in size from 0.5 - 3.5 m and may weigh  up to 4000 kg. This maintenance work is an essential part of resource operations. The maintenance departments on many sites have teams working 7 days a week, with maintenance work scheduled on both day and night shifts. This case study involves an incident that took place during maintenance  work.  
A fitter, Eric, and trainee fitter, Frank, were at work during night shift at the Batwing site. Their work  was to remove the old material such as lagging or liners from the units. Frank was using hand tools  and a hydraulic device to lever the lagging/liners from a unit. Eric was operating a forklift specially  designed to move these units. They were working outside on a concrete pad. The concrete pad was  a temporary arrangement for a couple of months while a new workshop was being built at the site.  
Both Eric and Frank work for a well-established contractor, RepairMax. Eric has 15 years resource  industry and maintenance experience and 4 months at this site. Frank has been on site for only a  week. He is new to the resource industry and to shift work and has no prior experience working on  this type of unit. At the respective starts of their employment at RepairMax, both men completed a  6 week contractor training course run by RepairMax. Both men had also received one day site-specific inductions for the Batwing site.  
At 18:00 on 12th April, Frank and Eric attend a pre-start information (PSI) meeting with the Batwing  Operations team. Mike, the site shift supervisor, talked about an incident the previous night shift in  which an interaction had occurred between two service trucks. Mike reiterated the requirement that should an incident occur, the scene of the incident should be preserved. Eric and Frank had problems hearing the pre-start talk as they were standing off to the side. This was the first time they would be working together.  
Following the PSI meeting, Eric completed a vehicle pre-start inspection for the forklift. He noted that the reverse lights were not working. Eric and Frank started work at about 19:00. Their first action was to complete a Take 5 assessment for the task they were assigned, removing liners from units. A Take 5 is a quick (5 minute) assessment of the risks involved in a task and the controls to be used for those risks. They then worked on the repair pad until lunch at 22:45 and returned there after lunch.
