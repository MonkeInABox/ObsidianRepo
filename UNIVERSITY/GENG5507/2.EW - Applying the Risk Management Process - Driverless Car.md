### Implementing a Risk Management Plan
#### Scope, Context, Criteria
- What are the objectives (scope) of the trial?
	- Measurable
	- Clearly described
	- I.e. monitor the driverless vehicle capabilities (hazard avoidance, response time, etc) and search for areas of improvement
		- Investigate the most efficient route for a year in order to test fuel consumption, time taken
- **UWA Campus, student and staff will test on site and slightly outside for about 12 months.**
- Context establishment
	- Organisation articulates objectives
	- Defines the external and internal parameters to be taken into account when managing risk
	- Develops risk criteria
	- From whose perspective are we developing the objectives? UWA or Google?
		- Considering UWA strategic priorities
			- Which would work for our priorities
		- Google will also have priorities, but they will more be objectives
	- External Context
		- The environment in which the organisation seeks to achieve its objectives
		- Stakeholder management
		- Social, cultural, political, legal, regulatory, financial, technological, economic, competitive environment
		- Key drivers and trends
- **External stakeholders: Neighbourhood (around outside of UWA), local, state governments, politicians, Main Roads, QEII patrons, la polizia, councils, insurance agencies, cyclists, local residents, original equipment manufacturers (OEMS).**
	- Internal Context:
		- Anything that can influence the way the organisation will manage risk
		- Objectives and criteria
			- Policies, capabilities, IT systems, relationships with internal stakeholders, culture, contracts
- **Internal Stakeholders: Staff, students, student guild, admin, business owners on campus, regular visitors, contractors.**
#### Risk Assessment
- Risk Identification
	- Find, recognise and describe risk that might help or prevent an organisation achieving objectives
		- Generate a comprehensive list of risk based on these events
	- Identify stakeholder groups
		- Brainstorming, structured techniques (like HAZID), scenarios
	- Using historical records, expert judgement, focus groups and surveys, insurance claims, post event reports, inspections, audits, site visits
	- **Risks: getting run over, run test only at specific hours limiting test, sensor failure, loss of power, reputational risk.**

# FMEA
- Technical processes used by engineers to identify, rank and control risks in the DESIGN PHASE
- Risk assessment > engineering risk assessment > examples of different risk assessment methods
- FMEA is a good illustration of the steps in *Figure 4 - Process* in "ISO 31000"
- FMEA is a systemised group of activities to:
	- Recognise and evaluate potential failures and effects
	- Identify actions which could eliminate or reduce the chance of a potential failure occurring
	- Document the process
$$RPN = L \times C \times D = O \times S \times D$$
1. Define scope
2. Define level of analysis
3. Identify functions and failure models
4. Identify causes of failure
5. Identify effects and severity rating
6. Assign frequency (occurrence) rating
7. Identify controls and assign detection rating
8. Calculate risk priority number for each effect
9. Rank failure modes for action
10. Rank failure modes for action and analysis
11. Return to 1.
- Use an FMEA risk matrix
- Higher $RPN$ carries a higher risk
- Black swans when they have very high severity but low chance of occurrence
