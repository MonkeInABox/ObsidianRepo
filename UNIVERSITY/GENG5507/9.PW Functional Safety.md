## Demand on Safety Functions
- During normal operations, machines have safety functions that are expected to either operate in
	- Low demand - less than once a year activation
	- High/continuous demand - more than once a year activation
- IEC 62062 and ISO 13849 only designs safety function in High Demand
## High and Low Complexity
- Complexity of components
	- It depends on the Failure Mode
	- High complexity - Failure modes of individual components cannot be completely defined
		- Safety PLC, microprocessor
	- Low complexity - Failure modes of individual components are well defined
		- Mechanical devices, relays, contactors

## SIL 
![[Pasted image 20260428122116.png]]
- For electrical/electronic/programmable systems
- Low demand
	- PFD - Probability of failure on demand
- High demand
	- PFH - Probability of failure per hour
## Risk Reduction Factor (RRF)
- Normally used in low-demand-mode
- It is the inverse of the required probability of failure on demand
	- E.g. $RRF = 10$ is a probability of failure of $0.1$
	- $RRF = \frac{BaselineRisk}{ModifiedRisk} = \frac{1}{PFD_{avg}}$

## Common Misconceptions
- Meeting probability of a system failure (PFD/PFH) is sufficient
	- Need to follow selection of good components and minimum hardware fault tolerance
- High SIL as always better
	- Higher SIL - higher insurance premium, higher maintenance cost
- SIL can be achieved by device only
	- Safety functions require input/logic/output

# V-Model and Safety Life Cycle
## Random and Systematic Failures
- We need to address RANDOM and SYSTEMATIC failures
- Random - Occurring at random time
	- Example: Operator error with procedure, random component failure (e.g. light bulb blows)
- Systematic - Has a direct cause, eliminated by changing design, manufacturing or operational procedures, or relevant factors
	- Example: Design flaw, ineffective procedures, calculation error, incorrect specification
- Address these: SYSTEMATIC through the engineering lifecycle, RANDOM through engineering design

## Basic Rules for Safety-Related Systems
1. Safety functions are considered after other risk reduction measures (inherent safety, mechanical protection) have been applied. Safety functions are used to close risk gap
2. SIL of a safety-related systems is the risk gap
3. SIL $\geq$ 3 should not be encouraged. Additional risk reduction measures required - typically there's something wrong with the process
4. Safety related systems of SIL 2 or 3 can be replaced by combination of independent lower SIL, i.e. $SIL1 + SIL1 = SIL2; SIL1 + SIL2 = SIL3$ 
	1. All sensors, logic solver, final elements must be separate
