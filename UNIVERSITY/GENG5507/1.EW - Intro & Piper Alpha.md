## Understand Role of Engineers in Delivering Safe and Reliable Systems
- Challenges:
	- Fast pace of technological change
	- Reduced ability to learn from experience
	- Changing nature of accidents (digital systems and software)
	- Increasing complexity (interactive, dynamic and non-linear)
	- Complex relationships between humans and automation
	- Changing regulatory and public views of safety

- Where are we now in thinking about RSS?
	- Need to focus on building better systems
		- Including people, process, technology
	- Systems are dynamic and complex
	- Trade-offs: cost, risk, performance - this is asset management

- Hierarchy of Controls:
	- Elimination
	- Substitution
	- Engineering Controls
	- Administrative Controls
	- Personal Protective Equipment

## Identify Organisational, Social and Human Factors that Contribute to Safe Systems
- What is needed  for safe systems? (3 Capital Model)
	- Social Capital: leadership, culture, trust
	- Organisational Capital: processes and management systems
		- What we should do and when
	- Human Capital: peoples' skills, awareness, experience

## CASE STUDY: The Piper Alpha Disaster and Impact on Development of Safety Regulation
- What factors aggravated the event?
	- Design Factors:
		- Adapted to do gas as well
			- Gas worksites close to sensitive areas as they were retrofitted
		- Pressure valve hidden by machinery
		- Fire walls versus blast walls
		- Radio room able to be damaged by danger
			- Lack of backup radio room
		- Did not think fail safes through
			- Gas area close to oil area with easily rupturable walls
		- No backup  power system, requiring them to rely on their own gas
		- Pressure plate thickness not designed to hold the pressure
		- High pressure pipelines with no proper measures taken
		- Ability to take out full fire-fighting system easily by turning on the hoses too quickly
		- Inefficient ventilation in fire safety rooms
		- No return valve from the other rigs
	- Management System Factors:
		- Changes to the normal operating routines
		- Platform not shutdown, despite small gas leaks
		- Signed off permit without manager
			- Permit system degraded
		- Fire fighting system not properly managed
		- Prioritising cost
		- Two permits for the same item
		- Proper pump checks not done before starting
		- Lack of change of shift communications
		- Fire fighting communications left on manual for too long
		- No physical, or otherwise, locking off system
		- Manager continuing to pump oil despite not having communications
		- Waiting too long to hear from upper management
		- Ignoring the reports' warnings about dangers
		- Not specifically trained on what to do in that situation
	- Cultural Issues:
		- Packing up when work is not done and left in a appropriate spot
		- Permits only treated as a formality
			- Signing off yourself
		- Prioritising cost
		- Fire fighting system not properly managed
		- Safety was treated as a bureaucratic burden
		- Gas leaks notices but not dealt with
		- Supervisor time pressure
		- Pushing on issues to the next one higher up, wasting time so that they don't have to take accountability or responsibility
		- Complacency for the safety measures
	- Immediate Events:
		- Changes to normal routine
		- Permit separation, causing confusion, then wrongly signed and then missing
		- No safety checks when shifting pumps
		- Job overran so pressure valve was not replaced
		- Titan and Claymore not turning off their oil production
		- Smoke and fires over the helideck and blocking access to boats
		- Pumps from other rigs back to it
