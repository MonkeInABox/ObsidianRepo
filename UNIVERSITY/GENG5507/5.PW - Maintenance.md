# What is Maintenance
- The combination of all technical, administrative and managerial actions during the life cycle of an item intended to retain it in, or restore it to, a state in which it can perform the required function
## Maintenance Management System
- Established to:
	- Identify maintenance objectives and priorities
	- Develop maintenance management strategy (how will we meet objectives, who is responsible and over what time frame)
	- Execute the maintenance management strategy
	- Control, measure, improve
## Objectives
- Generally the following
	- Safety
	- Technical
	- Legal/mandatory regulations
	- Financial

# Maintenance Tactic Development
- Without a formal approach, most people over-estimate the proportion of age-related failures
## RCM = Reliability Centred Maintenance
- One method for determining the maintenance tactic (strategy) for equipment
- Other methods include
	- Manufacturer's recommendation (OEM)
	- Using the PM program from other similar equipment
	- Reverse RCM or PM optimisation
	- Based on gut feel and experience

## Aims
- Detect and correct incipient failures
- Reduce probability of failure
- Detect hidden failures
- Increase the cost-effectiveness

## Difference Between RCM and PMO (Reverse RCM)
- RCM generates list of failure modes from rigorous assessment of all functions, consideration of all functional failures and then an assessment of each failure modes that relate to each functional failure. Primary objective is to preserve asset function
- PMO generates list of failure modes from current maintenance program
## RCM Criticisms
- Time consuming and resource hungry
- Difficult to measure value
- Significant number of failure modes assigned a tactic of no scheduled as it is not feasible or cost effective
- Using RCM templates can result in errors as the primary and backup units may have different failure modes
- RCM focussed on developing PM activities, lose sight of proactive solutions
## PMO Criticisms
- Unless special steps taken, failure modes that have not happened may not be considered, resulting in unmitigated hazards
- PMO may not identify hidden failures
- Does not list a complete set of failure modes
- Assumes all failure modes associated with equipment are covered by the existing maintenance program
- Often focuses on maintenance workload reduction
## Issues with Both
- Neither process provides adequate protection against the consequences of equipment failure where two failures occur  simultaneously.  
• Few operations have a live-program where the FMEA analysis (regardless of the method) used is updated following new failures or improved understanding and experience. Often the analysis is put on a shelf and forgotten about.  
• Problems in the asset register and with data in the CMMS system can stall both processes.  
• Inexperienced analysts may propose a condition monitoring task without considering that a failure must give a warning and that warning must provide sufficient time for action (P-F interval).  
• Often the ‘forward thinking about what the problems could be’ does not agree with what problems are actually experienced.

# Considerations for Asset Maintenance Tactics
## Fixed Interval Task Selection
- Applicable when 
	- Conditional probability of failure starts rapidly increasing after a specified age
	- Most items survive to that age
	- We can state with some degree of certainty what that age is
![[Pasted image 20260325131916.png]]
## Run to Failure Task Selection
- Applicable only for failure modes without safety or environmental consequences when
	- It is cheaper in the long run to all the item to fail

## Failure Finding Task Selection
- Failure finding tasks are applicable only for failure modes with hidden failure consequences when:
	- It is possible to test the device without destroying it
	- It is possible to test most or all of the device or system
	- Performing the test does not significantly increase the risk of inducing the multiple failure we are seeking to avoid
## Redesign
- Training
- Process/Systems
- Operational and maintenance procedures/practices
- Procurement decisions

## Condition Monitoring Task
- Applicable when:
	- Clearly defined potential failure condition
	- The P-F interval can be reasonably defined
	- The task can be done at a frequency which permits effective action to avoid the consequences of the failure
	- The frequency of a condition monitoring task is determined by the P-F interval

# Method for Calculating Reliability Parameters with Censored Data
## Suspensions
- Suspensions or censorings are cases where the component was still functioning at the time data was obtained
- Effectively, the component was suspended whilst still in operational state
- Gives information on how long the component has operated without failing
## Deriving Median Ranks for Censored Data
- List order number $i$ of failed items
- List increasing ordered sequence of life values $(t_i)$ of failed items
- Against each failed item list the number of items which have survived to a time between that of the previous failure and this failure (or t=0 to this failure)
- For each failed item, calculate mean order number $i$ 
![[Pasted image 20260325134041.png]]
where n = sample size
- Calculate the median rank using $$r_{t_{i}} = \frac{i_{t_{i}} - 0.3}{n + 0.4}$$
