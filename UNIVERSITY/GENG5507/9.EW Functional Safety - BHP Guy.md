# Case Study
- Heavy robot, with many degrees of freedom
- Motor 1: Swinging/pivot
- Motor 2: Lunging
- Motor 3: Wrist
### Functional Safety Management Plan (FSMP)
- What do we need
	- Lifecycle, input and output
	- Competency
	- Roles and responsibilities
	- Management of functional safety
	- Actions/non-conformance
	- Management of change procedures
	- Design guidelines/standards
	- Functional safety assessment plan
	- Verification/Validation plan

### Safety Requirements and Safety Function
- Clear and concise statement about how the function is achieved:
	- De-energise robotic arm when personnel is detected in the work area. The arm must stop within 10 degrees of swing movement
		- This now introduces and generates other hazards
	- Must have a response time of 1 second
### Design
- How do we stop the operator from interacting with the robot
	- Guarding (mechanical/civil)
	- Programming of motion limits
- ***SAFETY FUNCTION*** - Input > Logic > Output
	- Aim is to create a safe state between human and robot arm
	- Input
		- Safety mats
		- Laser curtain
		- Emergency stop
		- Camera
		- Gate switch interlock
- Mode of operation: LOW or HIGH demand - HIGH demand, it happens every day
	- How often does the safety function activate
	- PFDavg or PFH - PFD for low demand, PFH for high demand
		- $PFH_{system} = PFH_{input} + PFH_{logic}+PFH_{output}$
	- Then we can match to SIL
### Build and Implement
- Once the design is approved, the safety function can be built
### Factory/Function Acceptance Test (FAT)
- Ensure whatever product is manufactured is as per design specification in the factory environment
- Sometimes not possible
### Site Acceptance Test (SAT)
- Check on site and retest
- Very different environment to the factory
- Dust, temperature, weather
### Operate and Maintain
- How do operators reset
- Handover to user
- Are the personnel trained and aware
	- Competency
	- Operating procedures/Maintenance procedures
- Maintenance requirements
# Common Misconceptions
- Higher SIL is always better
	- Higher upfront cost, higher maintenance
- SIL can be achieved by device only
	- Safety requirements require input, logic and output
- Meeting probability of system failure is sufficient
	- Systematic capability - selection of good components
# Future of Functional Safety
- Automated assets with functional safety
	- Currently just commands, does not have brain of its own
# System Safety
- Used to demonstrate adequacy of the risks of the system
- Relies on scientific, technical and operational skills to identify, understand and control the hazards
- Functional safety is a part of the overall safety of a system or piece of equipment that depends on automatic protection operating correctly in response to its inputs or failure in a predictable manner (fail safe)
