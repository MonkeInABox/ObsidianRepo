# QRA
- A systematic, model-based procedure for investigating the ways in which a system operates
- What can go wrong?
- How likely?
- If it happens, what are the consequences expected?

# Tools for quantitative Assessments
- Functional block diagrams
- FMEA/FMECA
- Logic Models (fault trees and event trees)

# QRA Using ETAs
1. Define objective 
2. Familiarise with the system
3. Identify Initial Events for event scenarios of interest
4. Model each scenario (could use an event tree)
5. Model the failures (could use a Fault Tree)
6. Collect data to quantify the scenarios
7. Perform the calculations
8. Rank the risk scenarios

# ETAs (Event Tree Analysis)
- The conditional probabilities at the nodes of the ET are statistical estimates and are multiplied together down the branch to get the probability
	- Based on observations, test results, expert judgement, assumptions, etc.
- These estimates should be defensible, not speculative. The probability estimates should be reached based on a consensus

# Societal Risk
- Using an F-N curve (Frequency of fatalities/Probability - Number of fatalities)
- Sometimes cut in half by diagonal in a tolerable/acceptable and non-acceptable zone (bottom left is usually tolerable/acceptable)
	- There is often also an ALARP section
- The first risk developed were those for individual risk
	- It was estimated that the lowest natural individual death of a young child in a developed child is 1:10,000 per year and the comparable risk is roughly 10 times more
	- For imposed risks to be significantly lower, the HSE specified risk tolerance criteria of 1:10,000 to 1:100,000 per year to meet ALARP
- Society is more averse to risks faced by groups of individuals than to a single persons
- Society is more averse to risks posed by new infrastructure than existing infrastructure

# New Orleans Levees and Hurricane Katrina
- Killed 1,392 people and caused $125 billion in damages around New Orleans - Aug 2005
- There are 560km of levees in New Orleans, assume 280 segments
	- What is the average annual $P_{f}$ of a segment?
		- Levees designed for $T_{return}\approx 100$ 
		- $\lambda = \frac{1}{T_{return}}=0.01$
		- Probability of overtopping in 50 years considering a 100 year return hurricane?
			- $P(x>0)=1-e^{-\lambda t} = 0.39 | \lambda = 0.01 (OneIn100)$
		- There is a significant probability of levee overtopping in any 50 year period for a single segment of the levee system
		- The 3-4 failures that occurred in New Orleans, even without overtopping indicate that the levees were not safe in some locations from other types of failure modes
		- What is the $P_{f}$ of the entire levee system
			- The probability of reliability of each levee
			- $R = R_{1}*R_{2}*R_{3}*R_{4}*...*R_{n}$
			- $P_{f}= 1-R$ 
- In comparison, the primary dikes protecting the Netherlands are set to heights corresponding between 2,000 and 10,000 year return periods
