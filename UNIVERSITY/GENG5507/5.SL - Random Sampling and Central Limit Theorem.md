# Problems with Census Approach
- Expensive
- Time
- Difficult to conduct
- Measurements may be destructive
- Impossible in some situations

> [!NOTE] Sampling
> Make observations on a subset (sample) of the population, then the results to the whole population

## How should a sample be selected?
- Should represent the population
- Inappropriate techniques can lead to bias in results
- Common biases:
	- Location bias
	- Time bias

> [!NOTE] Population
> The entire group of about which information is sought


> [!NOTE] Sample
> A subset of the population that is examined for the purpose of gathering information

# Random Sampling
- Each unit in the sample is picked at random from the population
- Every unit has a known probability of being included in the sample
- Chance mechanism used in the selection process
- Eliminates bias
- Also known as probability sampling

# Probability Model
- We want to measure quantity $X$ for units in a population, a population distribution
- A parameter is any quantity associated with a population distribution, usually a summary measure of the population
- A sample is a set of independent and identically distributed (IID) random variables having same distribution as $X$
- A simple random sample (SRS) is one which every unit in the population has the same probability of being selected
- All estimation and inference is based on the population model and the data
- A statistic is a random variable the observed value of which depends only on the observed data
