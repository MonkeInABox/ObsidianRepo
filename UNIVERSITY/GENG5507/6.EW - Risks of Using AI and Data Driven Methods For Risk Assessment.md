# Case Study - Steel Catenary Risers (SCRs) Reliability
- Background:
	- Offshore assurance and life-extension assessments for deep water developments
	- The field development includes 6 identical SCRs connecting the seabed flowlines to a floating facility
	- Total contract for riser system exceeds AUD 500 million with major implications for safety, environmental protection and production availability
- As part of the contract, the operator requires:
	- Target probability of failure within 10 years for an SCR $P(T\leq 10) \leq 0.15$
	- Justification of any data-driven or AI based models
- There is a Touchdown point (TDP) where the SCR exits the Catenary Zone to the Buried Zone
![[Pasted image 20260402141104.png|660]]
- Each SCR consists of:
	- Carbon steel with welded girth points
	- Touchdown zone (TDZ) subject to high cyclic stresses
	- Corrosion protection system
	- Interfaces with seabed flowlines and floating structure
- SCR is considered to have failed if any one of the following:
	- Fatigue crack growth leading to rupture
	- Local buckling or collapse
	- Loss of containment due to corrosion
	- Structural failure during extreme environmental loading
- Each SCR is treated as a single critical component, with no redundancy
- Available Historical Field Data
	- 18 total SCRs operating over the past 15 years
		- 4 experienced fatigue related damage
		- 1 SCR experienced premature corrosion-related wall loss
		- The remaining SCRs have survived without reported failure
	- Times to failure (years)
		- 1: 6.5
		- 2: 8.2
		- 3: 11.0
		- 4: 14.5
		- 5: 9.0
	- The surviving SCRs have accumulated 15 years of service and are treated as right-censored observations
	- Scarcity of data!
	- A team proposes augmenting reliability assessment using AI and ML models trained on:
		- Historical SCR failure data
		- Monitoring data
		- Simulated response datasets from previous projects
	- Proposes to provide an additional 50 data points
	- Concerns
		- Limited failure data
			- May overfit sparse or biased datasets
		- Censoring and survivorship bias
			- AI may misinterpret survival as low risk rather than insufficient exposure time
		- Loss of physical meaning
		- Extrapolation risk
			- Data not valid for every different scenario
		- False confidence

# What is Wrong with Simulated Data
- Inconsistent definitions of damage metrics
- Limited traceability
- Biased training data
- Environmental inputs
- AI trained only on failures implicitly ignores survival information
- Survivorship bias leads to pessimistic or optimistic $P_f$ estimates
- Censoring often mishandled or ignored in black-box models
- Apparent accuracy masks epistemic uncertainty
- All models are wrong but some are useful
- Ignoring censored data biases the reliability estimation
- Sample bias
- Using erroneous simulations output - garbage in garbage out!

> AI outputs should be benchmarked against physics-based Weibull reliability
> Weibull fitting must use censored data
