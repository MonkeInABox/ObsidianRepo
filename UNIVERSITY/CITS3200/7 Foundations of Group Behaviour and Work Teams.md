# Formal and Informal Groups
- Formal: defined by organisations structure with designated work assignments establishing tasks
- Informal: neither formally structured nor organisationally determined
# Group Development
- Tuckman Model: Forming, storming, norming, performing and adjourning
### Group Properties
- Roles
	- Expected behaviours associated with a given position in the group
	- Conflict experienced when multiple roles are incompatible
	- Role expectations are how you should act in a situation
- Status
	- 
- Norms
	- Accepted standards of behaviour within group
	- Performance norms
	- Appearance norms
	- Social arrangement norms
	- Resource allocation norms
- Cohesive
	- Degree to which members are attracted to the group and want to stay
	- Influenced by: time spent together, small group size, shared experiences, competition, rewards shared
- Size
	- Socially defined rank within a group or society, which influences behaviour, identity and group dynamics
	- Group size matters
	- Social loafing
		- Reduce with high work ethic, conscientiousness and set a strong group identity and shared goals
- Diversity
	- Support innovation not uniformity
	- The degree to which other members of the group are similar
	- Early stages include more conflict, lower cohesion and satisfaction
	- Long term: greater creativity, openness and problem solving
### Group Decision Making
- Group vs. Individual Decision Making
	- Group: 
		- More information and input
		- Greater diversity
		- Better at creative alternatives
		- Time consuming
		- Conformity pressure
		- Domination by vocal members
		- Ambiguous responsibility for outcomes
- Groupthink is where the norm for consensus overrides the realistic appraisal of alternative courses of action
	- Lower decision quality
	- Less innovation
	- Suppression of learning
	- Minimised by: limiting group size, encourage impartial leadership, appoint devil's advocate, use structured decision-making techniques
- Groupshift is when decisions get more extreme than initial individual positions
	- Can shift towards greater risk or greater caution
	- Due to comfort with group, diffusion of responsibility, desire to differentiate from out-groups
# Understanding Work Teams
### Why so popular?
- Combine complementary skills
- More flexible and responsive
- Foster collaboration
### Teams Vs. Groups
- Work Group:
	- Interacts to share info
	- Help individual performance
	- No joint effort required
	- Output = Sum of individual components
- Work Team:
	- Coordinates effort for shared goals
	- Achieve collective performance
	- Mutual accountability
	- Output > Sum of individual efforts
- Teams are groups, groups are not teams
### Types of Teams
- Problem-Solving
	- Same department meet regularly to improve processes
	- Rarely have authority to implement
- Self-managed
	- Take on responsibilities of former supervisors
	- Plan, schedule, assign tasks, sometimes even evaluate performance
- Cross-Functional
	- Members from different departments
	- Diverse expertise
	- Often temporary or project-based
- Virtual
	- Use technology to collaborate
	- Highly flexible, but face challenges in trust and communication
- Multi-team systems
	- Work toward a shared goal, each team on a subtask
	- Designed for complex, fast moving environments
	- Encourages specialisation and integration
### Effective Teams
- More than just performance
	- Productivity quality, manager or stakeholder ratings
- Context, composition and processes
- Context: adequate resources, leadership and structure, climate of trust, performance evaluation and rewards
- Composition: abilities of members, role allocation, diversity and preferences, team size, member preferences, personality factors (extroversion, emotional stability, agreeableness, openness to experience, conscientiousness {called the BIG FIVE})
- Processes: Potential group effectiveness + process gains - process losses = actual group effectiveness
	- Common purpose and plan
	- Specific goals
	- Team efficacy
	- Team identity
	- Cohesion
	- Mental Models
	- Conflict levels
	- Social loafing
### Individuals --> Team Players
- Selection (hiring for team orientation)
- Training (developing team players)
- Rewarding (reinforcing team oriented behaviours)

### Always Good?
- Teamwork isn't free
- If work is simple, if goals are individual, if members are not interdependent; don't use teams
- When work is complex, requires a shared goal, diverse perspectives; use teams
