# Non-Conservative Forces
- Forces that you can not measure at any two points and the energy will be equal are conservative
- Forces that dissipate energy such as friction, air resistance or a dampener are non-conservative forces and therefore should be added to the "right side" of the equations47
# Action and the Lagrangian
- The action is the shortest path between points (the path of stationary action)
- For a single particle on a line $$T=0.5m\dot{x}^{2}, V=V(x)$$
- The action of a trajectory is $$A=\int^{t_1}_{t_0}(T-V)dt=\int^{t_1}_{t_0}(0.5m\dot{x}^{2}-V(x))dt$$
- This is the integral of the Lagrangian
- For a stationary action $$\delta A = 0$$
> "To do you a flavor"
- The Lagrangian equations we have been looking at are more accurately called the *Euler-Lagrange Equations*
# More particles and More Dimensions
- All together there are $N$ coordinates we call $x_{i}$ $$L=\sum\limits_{i} (0.5m_{i}\dot{x_{i}})-V(x)$$
- The action is also the integral of the Lagrangian
