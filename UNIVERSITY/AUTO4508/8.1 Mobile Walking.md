# Biped Walking Methods
- Static Balance
	- Centre of mass is always over support area of feet
	- Simple
	- Robot is continuously balancing on one single leg
	- Usually results in slow gait
- Dynamic balance
	- Centre of mass may be outside support area of feet during gait phases
	- Complex, either good timing or sensor feedback
	- Usually results in faster, more natural looking gait

# Zero Moment Point
- ZMP is the ground contact point of the robot where all the moments cancel each other out
- ZMP is the dynamic equivalent of the C.O.M. 
- Requires foot pressure sensors and accelerometers/gyroscopes
- Requires calculation of forward/inverse kinematics for positions and velocities/moments
- Widely published
# Inverted Pendulum
- Treat the robot as an inverted pendulum
- Requires inclinometers/gyroscopes
# PID Control
- Start with a rough pre-programmed gait that is parameterised for:
	- Step length
	- Height of leg lift
	- Walking speed
	- Amount of forward lean of torso
	- Maximal amount of side swing
- Use sensor feedback and PID controller to achieve desired side lean and forward lean in particular gait phase
# Artificial Horizon
- Visual feedback 
- Use a straight line in the robot's field as feedback for its orientation
- If on-board controller is fast enough, general optical flow can be used - no artificial line required
