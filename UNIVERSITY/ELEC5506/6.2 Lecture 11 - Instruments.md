# Using Instruments
- Accuracy: the degree of conformity of an indicated value to a recognised accepted standard value or ideal value
- Measured accuracy: maximum positive and negative deviation observed in testing a device under specified conditions and by a specified procedure
- Accuracy rating: a number or quantity that defines a limit that errors will not exceed when a device is used under specified operating conditions
- Systematic error: an error that in the course of a number of measurements made under same conditions of same value of a given quantity, either remains constant in absolute value or sign or varies according to a definite law when the conditions change
- Instrument range: capability of instrument
- Calibration range: he region between the limits within which a quantity is measured, received, or transmitted, expressed by stating the lower and upper range values. For example, 0 to 150◦ C. It differs to the instrument-range. For example a pressure gauge might operate between 0-50 Mpa and have an output of 4-20 mA. However, the instrument may only be calibrated between 20 and 30 mPa. The calibration-range is then 20-30 mPa = 4-20 mA. The zero input is 20 MPa and zero output is 4 mA. The input span is 10 mPa and output span is 16 mA.
- Span: algebraic difference between upper and lower range values
- Sensitivity: ratio of change in output magnitude to the change of input that causes it once steady state is reached
- Repeatability: the closeness of agreement among a number of consecutive measurements of the output for the same value of input under same operating conditions, approaching from the same direction, for full range traverses
- Reproducibility: The closeness of agreement among repeated measurements of the output for the same value of input, made under the same operating conditions over a period of time, approaching from both directions.
- Resolution: degree to which equal values of a quantity can be discriminated by the device
- Tolerance: permissible deviation from a specified value
- Traceability: all calibrations should be traceable to some recognised standard The ANSI/NCSL Z540-1-1994 standard (which replaced MIL-STD-45662A) defines traceability as“the property of a result of a measurement whereby it can be related to appropriate standards, generally national or international standards, through an unbroken chain of comparisons.”

# Communications Protocols
- 4-20mA current loop
	- The 4-20 mA current loop has the following advantages:
		• It is the dominant standard in many industries.
		• It is simple to connect and configure.
		• It only requires two wires reducing costs. See figure 7.7.
		• Good for long distances between transmitter and receiver because the current value does not decrease with distance provided enough voltage is applied to overcome the line resistance. See figure 7.8.
		• It is less sensitive to common mode electrical noise.
		• Since 4 mA is equal to the zero value, faults in the cable can be detected![[Pasted image 20260331092120.png]]
- CompoBus: There are two modes to this protocol, high-speed and long-distance. High-speed communication mode is 750 kbps at a max cable distance of 100 m. Long-distance communication mode is up to 500 m of cable length at speeds of 93.75 kbps. Omron is the only manufacturer of this protocol.
- DeviceNet: This is a digital network used to connect to simpler devices. It provides power along with communication lines and utilizes CAN (Controller Area Network), the same network technology used in vehicles. Both DeviceNet and Ethernet/IP use CIP (Common Industrial Protocol) which is managed by ODVA (https://www.odva.org/Technology-Standards/Common-Industrial-Protocol-CIP/Overview).
- Ethernet/IP: Not to be confused with TCP/IP (Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol) which is what devices such as a computer uses to connect to the internet. Ethernet/IP (Industrial Protocol) uses the Ethernet structure to connect with various devices. Depending on how much the programming software does for you, this might require a steeper learning curve compared to other protocols
- EtherCAT: Developed by Beckhoff Automation, EtherCAT is a real-time Industrial Ethernet technology focused onshort cycle times (≤ 100 us). Each device reads the data in its frame and inserts data to the frame as it travels downstream. Data streams can be as small as a couple bits and up to kilobytes information.
- RS-232C: Commonly found on older computers and was used for connecting printers, scanners, keyboards, etc. because of the cable length and device to device restrictions. It provided a single-ended channel with baud rates up to 20kbps, and later enhanced to 1Mbps. The C refers to the revision of this protocol. The primary difference between revisions is the voltage: A being 25V; B is 12V; and C is 5V. In 1962, revision C included other RS-232 specifications such as nominal ±5 V transmit and ±3 V receive (space/mark) signal levels, 2 V common-mode rejection, 2200 pF maximum cable load capacitance, 300 Ω maximum driver output resistance, 3 kΩ minimum receiver (load) impedance, and 100 ft (typical) maximum cable length. RS-232 systems are point-to-point only. With the development of RS-422 and RS-485, RS-232C is slowly being phased out but is still around to accommodate pre-installed systems.If the protocol is not specified or listed then it is assumed to be RS-232 revision C.
- RS-422: RS-422 is a unidirectional, full-duplex standard applicable to electrically noisy industrial environments and uses a single driver or master (100 Ω maximum driving resistance) with multiple receivers (minimum input impedance of 4 kΩ. The devices operate in differential mode devices and have a maximum baud rate is 50 Mbps and longer distances compared to RS-232C. This advancement also allowed up to 10 devices to be connected to a single driver. It was short lived and is now replaced by RS-485. The receiver common-mode range is ±7V, the driver output resistance is 100Ω maximum, and the receiver input impedance can be as low as 4 kΩ.
- RS-485: RS-485 is a half-duplex circuit. Requests and responses appear on the same 2-wire circuit. RS-232 is a full duplex circuit. Requests and responses appear on a different circuits. This protocol is able to achieve a multi-point network with multiple devices and drivers running at the same time. RS-485 can also be used to control some RS-422 devices. The RS-485 Standard was developed to overcome the weakness of RS-232 and RS-422. RS-485 is a bidirectional, half-duplex with a minimum receiver input impedance is 12 kΩ, and minimum driver output voltage of ±1.5 V and maximum of ±5 V. The standard allows for 32 receivers in parallel (minimum load of 375 Ω) with two 120 Ω terminations with 24AWG twisted pair connections. It also meets all RS-422 specifications.

# Temperature Measurement
- Negative Temperature Coefficient (NTC) thermistor A thermistor is a resistor that exhibits a large and repeatable change in resistance as a function of temperature. An NTC thermistor provides a very high resistance at low temperatures. As temperature increases, the resistance reduces. The output of a NTC thermistor requires linearization. The effective operating range is -50 to 250 ◦C for glass encapsulated thermistors or 150◦C for standard devices.
- Resistance Temperature Detector (RTD) An RTD measures temperature by the change in resistance of the RTD element with temperature. The most accurate RTDs are made using platinum but lower-cost RTDs can be made from nickel or copper and operate across -200 to 600◦C. RTDs are expensive compared to thermocouples. The resistance of RTDs increases with temperature.
- Thermocouple A thermocouple consists of a junction of two wires of different metals and by the Seebeck effect produces a voltage if there is a temperature gradient. Thermocouples are nonlinear and require conversion when used for temperature control and compensation but can operate across wide temperature ranges, from -200◦C to 1750◦C depending on the thermocouple type.
- Semiconductor-based sensors These devices can be very useful but have limited temperature ranges depending on the semiconductor used
