# Alarm Management

> [!EXAMPLE] Texaco, Milford Haven (1994)
> In the last 11 minutes before the explosion the two operators had to recognise, acknowledge and act on 275 alarms
- Alarms can be easily configured, cheap and easy
- Proper consideration is *seldom* given to alarm configuration during design and implementation phase
- The attitude is that more alarms are better
	- Information overload

> [!NOTE] Definition: Alarm
> An alarm is an audible and/or visible means of indicating to the operator an equipment malfunction, process deviation or abnormal condition requiring a (timely) response.

ISA 18.2 - Objectives
![[Pasted image 20260324014237.png]]

- Some alarms are not actually alarms
![[Pasted image 20260324014328.png]]
- Key design principles
	- Every alarm should have a defined response
	- Adequate time should be allowed for the operator to carry out a defined response
	- Every alarm presented to the operator should be useful, relevant and unique
	- Each alarm should alert, inform and guide
	- Alarms should take into account human limitations
## Characteristics of a Good Alarm
1. Relevant: not spurious or of low operating value
2. Unique: not duplicating another
3. Timely: not long before any response is needed or too late to do anything
4. Prioritised: indicating the importance that the operator deals with the problem
5. Understandable: having a message which is clear and easy to understand
6. Diagnostic: identifying the problem that has occurred
7. Advisory: indicative of the action to be taken
8. Focusing: drawing attention to the most important issues
### Alarm Prioritisation
- Alarm priority tells the operator which alarms to respond to first
- The relative importance assigned to an alarm within the alarm system to indicate the urgency of response
	- The severity
	- The time available

### Alarm Suppression Design Techniques
- Static suppression - operator initiated suppression of an alarm or group
	- Recommended for groups of alarms linked to equipment that is out for service or for chattering alarms
- Dynamic suppression - logic initiated suppression in a controller based on current or expected process status (suppression by design)
	- E.g. low flow alarm suppressed if pump stopped/valve closed
