- Pierre Schaeffer at French Radio (RTF) experimenting with tape
	- Etudes de Bruits noise studies
	- Splicing cutting, looping and superimposing ideas
	- Creates Groupe de Recherche de Musique Concrete (GRM) in 1951
- Tomorrow Never Knows - The Beatles
	- Producer George Martin revolutionised thinking around the studio and recording
	- Studio as an instrument
		- Tape splicing
		- Playback speed and direction manipulations
- Music for Airports - Brian Eno
	- Uses multiple running tape loops to create generative ambient music
	- Loops are different lengths, so overlap uniquely over longer periods of time
- Adventures on the Wheels of Steel - Grandmaster Flash
	- Extreme sampling of other songs
- Fight the Power - Public Enemy
	- Quintessential example of sampled breaks with rap and DJing
- The Robots - Kraftwerk
	- Almost purely synthesised
- Hardware through this period starts to become popularised as a consumer market
- Sequencers and synthesisers - all communicating over their own proprietary protocols
- Ten Ragas to a Disco Beat - Dharanjit Singh
	- Jupiter-8 keyboard
	- TR-808
	- Synchronised all the instruments together through the keyboard
- Xtal - Aphex Twin
	- Quintessential 90s early IDM/techno/electronic music
	- Roland SH-101
	- Korg MS-20
	- Yamaha DX7
	- Connected together via an Atari 520ST
- 2001 - Ableton Live version 1.0
	- Plays different audio samples with warping
		- One of their unique features that no-one yet has
	- In 2004, version 4.0 gets full MIDI support
