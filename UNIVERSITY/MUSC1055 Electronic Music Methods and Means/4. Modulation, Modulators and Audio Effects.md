# Signal Flow
- Signals in live flow through the software
	- They have an origin and destination
	- They can also break off and create new streams (routing)
	- We can also transform the stream as it flows (effects)
# Audio Effects
- Hardware or software tools that transform the textural, spatial and dynamic properties of a signal or sound
- Types:
	- EQ/filter - Remove or boost specific frequencies
		- Common parameters:
			- Frequency - where the filter is applied in the spectrum
			- Resonance/Q - the instensity of the filter around the cutoff frequency
			- Filter Type - what shape filter type should be used
		- Low/high pass, low/high shelf, notch, bandpass, experimental combinations - comb/vowel
		- Static filtering - changing the overall characteristic of a sound, make it warmer/brighter/shrill
		- Dynamic filtering - create movement in a sound, spectral fade in, attach the filter to an envelope
	- Time and modulation - Extend and transform the temporal qualities
		- Delays, repeats and shifts douns in time to create novel effects such as echo, reverb and more
		- Time and modulation effects are largely built on the foundation of delays
			- E.g. reverb can be implemented as a network of cascading delays
		- Delays:
			- Common parameters: dry/wet (balance of delayed and unprocessed signal), time (how long betwen delay iterations), feedback (how much delayed signal is put back into the delay)

	- Dynamics - Manipulate the dynamics
		- Control the level of the signal
		- Change the difference between the loudest and softest parts of a sound
		- Compression:
			- Listens to and follows the amplitude envelope of a sound
			- Based on the envelope, it attenuates the volume
				- Threshold
				- Ratio
				- Attack
				- Release
				- Makeup
		- Sidechaining:
			- Process one track, but receive input from another
			- Often used with compression
	- Distortion - Add harmonics
		- Degrades, warps or adds harmonics to a sound
		- Began as error that became aestheticised
		- Emulates analogue equipment
	- Spatial - Change the location of a sound in a mix
		- Change the source location of a sound
		- Modifying the relative level of each speaker producing the sound to give the impression it is produced from a specific location in the stereo field
	- Spectral - Control the spectrum of sound
		- Represents the strength of individual frequencies, as opposed to sound pressure, which provides more fine-grained control over the textual characteristics
# Racks and Macros
- Racks are a way to combine multiple instruments or effects together
	- They provide mechanisms for controlling them in creatively productive ways
	- They are a great organisational tool for your sound
	- Two vignettes: instrumental layering and spatial effect processing
# Modulators
- Apply an offset to a parameter
- Two modes - bipolar or unipolar
	- Polarity determines which direction modulation is applied from the current parameter value
	- Unipolar is in one direction (positive or negative)
	- Bipolar is in either direction simultaneously
- Intensity is expressed as a percentage
- Low frequency oscillator (LFO)
	- LFO generates a periodic control signal that can be mapped to any other parameter
	- Typically, the LFO oscillates in a sinusoidal pattern, but there are other options
	- It is important to understand the concept, rather than the specific device
- Envelope followers
	- Tracks the amplitude of a signal and maps the shape to a parameter
	- You can control the rise/fall to make it smoother, and amplify or attenuate the signal
	- Useful tool for coupling an effect to the intensity of the sound
