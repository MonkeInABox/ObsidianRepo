# Hermann von Helmholtz and the First Sound Synth (1863)
- His book 'Sensations of Tone'
- Deals with sounds - its physical properties and our perception
- Explains tone and timbre in terhms of physical phenomena
- Showed that differences in timbre of musical instruments and also sung/spoken vowel sounfs is a result of the differences of their overtones (partials)
- Designed apparatus - first synth
- Helmholtz's  apparatus essentially performs additive synthesis - the construction of sounds by the addition of several tones
# Sinewaves / Sinetones
- Simplest
- Produced by vibrating matter causing disturbances in the air received by ears as air pressure
- Sinusoid
- Two essential components
	- Frequency (pitch)
	- Amplitude (loudness)
# Freq and Amp
- Freq - rate at which vibrating mass produces a cycle of positive neutral and negative air pressure
	- A cycle - number of cycles within a second = frequency = Hz
- Amplitude - amount of positive and/or negative air pressure (displacement) from neutral
# Timbre / Tone Colour
- Sinetones are artificial
- All other sounds have several frequencies, a fundamental and several overtones
- Fundamental frequencies is the pitch of the sound
	- The overtones give the timbre or tone colour - with its relative amplitudes
- Sounds with a clear sense of pitch have a waveform with a clear repeating pattern
	- In these sounds we will find successive overtones at whole number multiples of the fundamental
- Helmholtz theory: combining sinetones together at equally spaced frequencies, we can then adjust each of their amplitudes independently to create an infinite variety of timbres / tone colours and therefore we can synthesis sound
# Additive synthesis
- Mutliple components, freq, amp and phase
- The lowest frequency is the fundamental
	- Above are upper partials, harmonics or overtones
- Adding sinewaves together in different combinations produces different composite waveforms and therefore different timbres
# The Telharmonium
- Thaddeus Cahil USA 1897 - 1912
- Attempted to generate realistic musical timbres using multiple tone wheels
- Later versions weighed over 200 tons and required entire rooms
- Music can be transmitted over telegraphic wires to multiple locations at once
- Attracted large commercial interest and investment
# The Theremin
- Leon Theremin in 1920s Russia
- Government funded research project into proximity sensors
- Two antennae: one to control pitch the other volume
- Patented in 1928 USA
# Pierre Schaeffer
- Paris 1948 - Composes and broadcasts Cinq Etudes de Bruit at Radio France
- Coined the term Musique Concrete - use of every day sounds to create music
- Was the first to manipulate recorded sounds to be used as music
- Noise etudes are the earliest examples of sound collage
- Earliest example of the loop in electronic music, using locked grooves on phonograph record discs
- 1951 - Conducts some of the first experiments with tape for musical purposes - looping and splicing
# Tape Studios
- In the 50s studios like Radio France (Paris) and WDR (Cologne) studios moved primarily to tape
- The advent of tape tech opened up many new creative possibilities for electronic musicians
	- Cutting and splicing
	- Speed changes
	- Reversing
	- Over-dubbing (layering)
	- Looping
- The first drum loops were put together and played back live in clubs on reel-to-reel tapes
# Karlheinz Stockhausen
- Paris 1952 - composes Konkrete Etude in Schaeffer's studio
- Cologne 1952 - Studio developed WDR by Herbert Eimert for Elektronische Musik - creating sounds from electronically synthesised sounds (additive synthesis) composed onto magnetic tape
- Studie 1 (1953) and Studie II (1954) at WDR
- Gesang der Junglinge (1956) combines Concrete and Elektronische 
- Stockhausen's work gained great public notoriety and influenced popular music and German Krautrock (back to Kraftwerk)
# Forbidden Planet (1956)
- Electronic tonalities composed by Bebe and Louis Barron
- First completely electronic film score
- Constructed own circuits
- Most sounds produced with either additive synthesis and a technique called ring modulation
- After creating sounds, further effects were applied such as tape delay and reverb, reversing and speed change
# Modular Synthesisers
- First commercial modular synth developed by Don Buchla and Robert Moog 1960s - flexibility and liver performance capability
- Buchla and Moog differed in their approaches and philosophy
	- B: Keyboardless, new approach ro music making with maximum flexibility
	- M: characterised by the Ladder Filter, with keyboard, providing access to regular musicians
- The Buchla 100 Electronic Music System (1963), the first Moog Modular (1963) and most modulars that followed have at least the following:
	- Oscillators
	- Envelope registers
	- Filter
	- Voltage controlled amplification
	- Noise generator
- Buchla Type
	- Pauline Oliveros - Once Again / Buchla Piece (1966)
	- Morton Subotnick - Silver Apples of the Moon (1967)
	- Pink Floyd - On the Run (1972)
- Moog Type
	- Wendy Carlos - Switched on Bach (1968)
# The Minimoog (1970 - 1981)
- Created in response to the use of modular synths in mainstream rock
- Integrated keyboard synths such as the Minimoog, rapidly became a standard instrument within mainstream rock from the early 70s, superceding the earlier modular synths
- The panel of the Minimoog can be broken into 3 sections:
	- Oscillator bank
	- Amplifier
	- Filter
	- Can all be modified by modulating oscillators and an envelope generator
