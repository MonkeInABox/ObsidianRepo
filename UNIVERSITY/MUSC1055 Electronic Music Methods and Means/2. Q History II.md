# Disco Sucks Fever
- Rampant commercialisation of disco in the late 70s, its nadir being the film and smash album Saturday Night Fever brought about a backlash from conservatives and the rock generation
- Chomisky Park baeball stadium on July 12th 1979 the site for a Disco Demolition Derby organised organised by Detroit DJ Steve Dahl
- The Disco Sucks phenomenon stemmed from a belief that disco was inauthentic, decadent and betrayed the true authentic American folk music, Rock and Roll
- In their embrace of Disco, clubs like Chicago's 'The Warehouse' and NYs 'Paradise Garage' went against the tide of the dominant culture who assumed disco to be an unfortunate mistake and well and truly over
	- Had to get music from Europe

# Proto-house - DJ Roots
- Frankie Knucles at the Warehouse, Radio WMBX The Hot Mix Five
- FK, DJ from NY span at the Warehouse in 1977, previously working at NYs Paradise Garage with DJ Larry Levan
- In 80s, no longer steady stream of new disco, FK would rework existing material into new forms
- Inspired by the innovation of DJs like FK, Hot Mix Five began to mix live but on radio (Steve Silk Hurley, Farley Jackmaster Funk)
- This post-disco/proto-house music was constructed largely out of existing musical material by montage, seque - essentially remixing, a more radical approach to techniques originating with disco DJs in the early 70s
- Existing material would often be disco club classics or from US soul and disco music record labels like Philadelphia International and Salsoul
# The Drum Track
- In time FK and others started using live drum machines as an extra rhythmic element - often reinforcing with a 4 to the floor kick pattern
- Most often made of a Roland drum machine - TR-707, TR-727, TR-808, TR-909
- Recorded on reel to reel, stock drum machine sounds as synthesised claps, kick, snare and hihat as common features
- With this final element, the house style emerged
	- re-edited, remixed and re-imagned disco records, reinforced by drum tracks with the ever present 4 to the floor kick
- Term in the early 80s to describe the type of music at the Warehouse
# Ron Hardy at "The Music Box"
- In 1982 FK opened his own club, the Power Plant, purists following him there
- The Warehouse relocated and became the Music Box
- 2 DJs playlists were quite similar:
	- FK sets served to extend the disco medium with great attention to sound quality
	- Hardy played a lo-fi faster more repetitive and stripped down version, ofen using EQ with pronounced effect
- The younger crowed were more attracted to the craziness of the Music Box, here the more radical sounds of house found a home

# House and Techno as Distinct Genres
- Within 450 km of each other
- Techno first used as late as 1988, to distinguish from house
- Despite proximity in time and plce, distinct in several respects:
	- Rhythm
		- Chicago house has a straight metronomic four-to-the-floor beat
		- Detroit was home to Motown funk, and techno artists did not escape this influence, the rhythm programming in techno tends to be complex, syncopated and groove-based
	- Vocals
		- In house, gospel or diva
		- Techno almost all instrumental or sampled/chopped
	- Intent/Attitude
		- House songs are necessarily human and emotionally expressive
		- Techno has a futuristic stance, and emphasises technology and machines over human beings
