# Planning Communication Management
- Information should be provided in the right format, at the right time, to the right audience with the right impact
- 75-90% of time spent communicating
# The Communication Process Model
- Source/Sender > Encoding > Channel message > Decoding > Receiver > Response/Feedback + Noise
## Barriers
- Lack of client involvement
- Poorly informed stakeholders
- Lack of meetings/too many meetings leading to little action
- Lack of reporting requirements
- Poor and incomplete documentation
- Frequent scope change
- Changing project personnel
- Lack of auditing the project to identify the lessons learned
# Managing Project Communication
- Timely and appropriate planning, collection, creation, distribution, storage, retrieval, managemetn, control, monitoring and disposal of information
- Fact - indisbutable, objective truth accepted by everyone
- Fantasy - someones opinion or interpretation
- Folklore - rumour, gossip
- Feelings - intuition, ego, emotion
# Project Meetings
- How much of this time is spent wisely, efficiently and effectively
- How many of the meetings result in actionable outcomes
- What percentage of meetings include stakeholders who make the required decisions
- How much constructive information is presented at the meetings
- How participative are the meetings
- How many meetings are poorly managed
- How many meetings are held to ratify what has already been decided
- How often do meetings go back over ground already discussed at previous meetings
# Kick-off Meeting
- Detailing the project objectives, expectations, deliverables, outcomes and benefits
- Meeting the client
- Reviewing the scope inclusions and exclusions
- Clarifying th roles and responsibilities
- Explaining the project management methodology
- Walking through the project management plan
- Discussing different views between the stakeholders and team members
# Kick-out Meeting
- Formally brings the project to a close
- Completed or not
- All aspects of the project are formally closed out
# Project Performance Report
- Can often lead to a work of fiction or a concise and honest summary of the projects progression, status and likely conclusion
- Should convey:
	- Accurate, complete and timely information
	- Provide a possible escape route for a mismanaged project
	- Reflect the true facts on the projects progression
	- Encourage the early detection of problems
	- Enable problem-solving and facilitate decision making
	- Track all the scope changes and revisions
	- Sustain the momentum, energy and commitment
# Reporting Continuum
- Progress Report (zero to present) - achievements, updates, issues, milestones
- Status Report (present) - on time, on budget as specced
- Forecast Report (future, completion)
# Change Control
- All scope change requests are to be in writing
- Must identify and be signed by the stakeholder initiating the change
- Must identify the complete impact
- Updated risk assessment
- Updated quality assessment
- Updated contract assessmetn
# Causes of scope creep
- Poor inital definions
- Unanswered quesitons
- Lack of stakeholder involvement
- Evolving expectations
- New solution discovery
- Ineffective project management
- Environmental factors
# Measuring Achievement
- Performance milestones
- Time
- Deliverabes (TBL)
- Effort
- Budget
- Duration
- Issues
- Risks
- Decisions
- Reviews
- Approvals
# Taking Corrective Action
- Behind schedule:
	- Accelerate
	- Increase resources
	- Reduce quality
- Over budget
	- Reduce quality 
	- Narrow scope
	- Accept substitution
- Outside scope:
	- Renegotiate costs
	- Redefine budget
	- Stop project
# Effective Project Control
1. Establishing standards
2. Monitoring standards
3. Measuring performance
4. Taking corrective action to correct deviations
- Define the acceptable range of variation
