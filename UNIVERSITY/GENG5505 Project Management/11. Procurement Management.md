# Planning Procurement Activities
- Processes necessary to purchase or acquire products or services or results
- Process-based function driven by a particular department or local division seeking to get possession of something that solves an operational issue
- Value-adding function that focuses on the efficient attainment of goods, services or results that deliver a number of very specific and measured benefits
- Make best possible use while supporting scope change
- TBL and life cycle thinking as the core of procurement 
# Procurement Drivers
- Supply the project with goods and services as specified and required
- Improved relationships with key suppliers
- Balance output with value and quality
- Increase accountability in supply chain
- Encourage greater access to innovative tech, premium materials, workplace competencies and expertise
- TBL and life cycle thinking as the project core

# SoW vs. Specifications
- SoW - defines the component that will be included in the project 
	- The WHAT
	- What the project wants
- Specification - defines the technical detail of the work to be performed
	- The HOW
# Suggestions for Improving Specification
- Clear understanding of scope and agreed spec
- Clear, complete and user friendly
- Express clauses and conditions clearly
- Include a glossary of terms
- State the evaluation criteria
- Include response templates
- Use a third party to review
- Include a copy of the proposed contract
- Encourage alternative/innovative responses consistent with TBL and life cycle approach

# Essential Contract Elements
- A contract is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties to act or refrain from acting in a particular way
- With the intent of protecting the interests of both parties, should contain as a minimum:
	- An offer
	- Acceptance
	- Intention/consent of each party to be legally bound
	- Consideration (something is given back for the promise)
	- Mutuality
	- Capacity (neither under duress)
	- Legality
- Inclusion of both express and implied terms, conditions and warranties
- Provision for breach, remedy and other non-performance redress

# Types of Contract
1. Fixed price 
	- Customer risks
		- At mercy of a sole source contractor
		- Incomplete specifications 
		- Tendency of the contractor to use cheaper materials
	- Contractor Risks
		- Careful cost estimation
		- Careful schedule estimation
		- Cost growth can lead to unprofitable projects
2. Cost-reimbursable
	- Seller paid for actual costs plus fee
	- Customer risks
		- Final cost unknown
		- Sole source contractor
		- Poor specifications leads to more costs
	- Contractor Risks
		- Fee percentage declines as costs rise
		- Rising costs can damage the relationship with customer
3. Times and material
	- Features of both fixed and cost plus
	- Customer risks
		- No specification
		- Open-ended
		- Sole source contractor
	- Contractor risks
		- Fixed rates
		- No defined tasks

# Controlling Procurement Activities
- Contract manager, administrator, superintendent or project manager
- Directing and managing authorised work
- Inspecting and verifying completed work prior to payment
- Ensuring all variations are properly assessed and approved
- Confirming rectification of all corrective work
- Maintaining accurate historical records

# Closing Procurement Activities
- Reviewing all procurement docs
- Updating and archiving all appropriate records
- Financial reconciliations
- Analysing all request and approved contract changes
- Completion of the agreed inspection and testing procedures
- Reporting and rectifying of any defects or damages
- Auditing the entire process
- Written confirmation that it is completed
