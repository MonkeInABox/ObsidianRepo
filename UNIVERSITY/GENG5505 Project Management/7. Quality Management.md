# The Challenges of Quality
- A personal dimension, framed by individual experiences and expectations
- Implies reliability, satisfies customer, value for money, complies with specifications
- Dictates measurement (if we cannot measure something, it makes it rather difficult to ensure conformance, compliance and ultimately improvement)
- Defined as a characteristic i.e. excellence, attainment
- Every project is created and managed to be successful as measured against some defining benchmark, most notably time achievement, deliverable compliance and cost control + TBL and lifecycle
- Project success is also measured against the clients identified agreed needs, not just their wants
- Clients not only have a technical specification, they also have an intangible expectation (often unstated) of quality against which the deliverable is constantly measured
# The Road to Quality
## Quality Planning
- All the mandated standards, op definitions and business requirements are clearly identified and agreed. It then aims to ensure that those same standards can in fact be achieved and measured throughout the project (quality is always planned into the project from the start, not simply randomly inspected in as required)
- E.g.
	- Reviewing scope doc, detailed product descriptions, tech specs
	- Examining all op procedures, relating to best practice and op efficiencies
	- Aligning with current business quality policy and supporting proceses
	- Designing a range of checklists that can be used to verify consistency
	- Accessing any standards and regulaitons pertaining to the project
- Building in quality through:
	- Budget cost
	- Clear specs
	- Defined standards
	- Historical experience
	- Qualified resources
	- Impartial reviews
	- Effective change control
	- Organisational wide commitement
## Quality Assurance
- A declaration or guarantee that the overall project performance (input, process, output) is evaluated on a regular basis to give all stakeholders the confidence that the relevant quality standards will be satisfied
- E.g.
	- Quality management plan
	- Business rules and op definitions
	- Appropriate internal systems
	- Processes to eliminate waste, variation and excess
	- Regular meetings
	- Scheduled audits
	- Lessons learnt
	- ISO standards
		- Quality Management
		- Environmental manegement
		- Social Responsibility
## Quality Control
- Monitors specific task and projectresults to identify, measure and eliminate the causes of unsatisfactory performance while ensuring that quality compliance is always demonstrated and achieved
	- Control implies measurement
- E.g.
	- Peer reviews
	- Physical inspection
	- Control charts
	- Checklists
	- Flowcharts
	- Cause and effect diagram
- Processes
	- Quality control monitors specific task and project results to identify, measure and eliminate the causes of unsatisfactory performance, while also ensuring that quality compliance is always demonstrated and achieved
	- Quality control is used through the implementation and finalisation stages of the project to formally demonstrate, with reliable data that acceptance criteria have been met
- Can deliver:
	- Elimination of rework
	- Confirmation of acceptance
	- Completed checklists
## Quality (continuous) Improvement
- A culture, a commitment and an ownership of what the project is delivering and ultimately how well it is being delivered
- Where innovation is encouraged, continuous improvement will flourish. Where efficiencies, economics of scale and capacity can be enhanced, continuous improvement will flourish. Where risk taking is supported, continuous improvement will again flourish
- E.g.
	- Regular performance reporting
	- Meetings and debriefs
	- Walkthroughs and peer reviews
# Balanced Scorecard (BSC) ***EXAM***
- Performance measurement framework which includes strategic performance metrics - i.e. customer perspective, project perspective, financial perspective, growth and innovation perspective - to give project managers and other key stakeholders a more balanced view of project performance
- Customer perspective - satisfaction, economic value added, intended objectives
- Financial perspective - within budget, variance between original budget and final budget, projct costs compared to industry standards
- Project perspective - team satisfaction, resource management
- Growth and innovation perspective - best practices identified, ongoing improvement, innovative ideas rate, TBL, life cycle
## BSC Throughout the Project
1. Initial measurement to establish a baseline for project planning
2. BSC benchmarks are included in the overall project plan
3. BSC measurements are implemented and initial benchmarks are used to compare and improve the BSC outcomes
4. BSC measurements reviewed and documented in final report to support best practices and for lessons learnt
# Indicators to Measure Sustainability Performance ***EXAM***
- Economic: will the project generate prosperity and enhance the affected economies?
- Social: Will the project be implemented in a socially responsible manner and benefit the affected communities in a fair and equitable way?
- Environmental: Will the project cause long-term damage to the environment?
- Natural resources: Will the project protect and enhance natural capital?
## 69 indicators
- Economic: governance (policy alignment), economic (jobs, tax), financial (profit), innovation, risk
- Social: governance, social infrastructure (public safety and security, local education, stakeholder engagement), employment (job security, safety), risk
- Environmental: governance, emissions (waste, pollution, hazardous material), risk
- Resources: governance, natural resources, risk
