# Approaches to Budgetting
- A budget is a formal written financial statement of management's plans for the future expressed in financial terms
- Should use **2 different** techniques
- Types:
	- **Traditional** - previous years level of performance is the foundation for next years figures
	- **Zero based** - ignores previous results as each activity and outlay is justified, each activity is recorded with zero spending to begin with
	- **Program** - activities are grouped together for projecting costs generated by each program or major activity
	- **Top-down** - based on pooling knowledge of senior managers and past resullts, project costs are estimated and then passed to lower level managers who continue to breakdown into further estimates
	- **Bottom-up** - individual task budgets are estimated in detail by the people directly responsible for doing or managing the work, estimates are aggregated to give the total project cost
# 2 Perspectives on Budgets
- Advantages:
	- Increase ability to improve decision-making processes
	- Effective means of cost control 
	- More reliable profits can be determined
	- Assess operational financial requirements
	- Ideal for areas needing control measures
	- Identify & diagnose problems
	- Act to improve management image
	- Useful in achieving long-term objectives
- Disadvantages:
	- Top to bottom support is difficult to get
	- Time spent planning can delay action
	- Often ‘locked’ & inflexible
	- Restrictive in terms of innovation & change
	- Can ignore cyclical fluctuations
	- Communicate unrealistic targets
	- Based on ‘guessed’ & unreliable estimates
	- Involve a high degree of uncertainty
	- Can be expensive to prepare

# The ROI of Sustainability
- Short term cost can be higher, long term evidence that there is good returns when TBL considered
	- Sustainability should be a core value of every company
- Operation cost category
	- Paper costs
	- Facilities costs
	- Energy costs
	- Waste costs
	- Packaging costs
	- Transport costs
- Factors driving sustainability
	- Desire for environmental/social stewardship
	- Desire to increase brand reputation
	- Obtaining competitive advantage
	- Internal/external stakeholder pressure/expectations
	- Rising energy costs
	- Regulatory compliance
- Challenges
	- Budget
	- Difficulty to demonstrate acceptable ROI
	- Fear of disrupting current business processes
- Trade-offs
	- Simultaneously manage social, environment and financial performance
	- Company's informal systems promote sustainability, their formal systems seemingly have a very traditional focus on financial performance
