# 3. Analysing the Risk
- Program evaluation and review technique (PERT analysis) considers the critical path
- SWOT and PESTELG analysis – consider issues impacting the situation
- Expert judgement – considers opinions, perspectives & viewpoints
- **Stakeholder forums** – considers expectations, objectives & deliverables
- Decision trees – considers possible paths & expected values returned
- Sensitivity analysis – considers demand, feasibility, take-up
- Impact analysis – considers action & consequences
- Scenario scheduling – considers alternative scheduling options
- Contingency planning – considers possible options & recovery strategies
- Financial modelling - considers financial implications for funding & cash flows
# 4. Managing the Risk: Possible Risk Responses
1. Reject - modify plan to eliminate risk
2. Accept - address as they arise
3. Mitigate - proactive action to minimise impact
4. Share - partnership with 3rd party
5. Transfer - outsource to 3rd party
6. Enhance - actively increasethe probability and or positive impacts of an opportunity
7. Exploit - ensure that the identified opportunity is realised by eliminating the uncertainty around it altogether so that the opportunity definitely happens
# 5. Monitoring and Evaluating - Documenting the Risk Register
- Risk event, Probability, Impact, Priority, Strategy, Responsibility, Review
- The more detailed the risk event, the more targeted the response
- Managed risk requires the ‘right’ owner
- Risk is always communicated and owned by all stakeholders
- Risk is either rejected, accepted or managed
- Risk can impact the project schedule and other variables
- Ongoing risk control is essential
- Risk register should be updated at every phase of the project lifecycle

# Aligning the Risk Profile
- Risk averse
	- Low tolerance & ability to accept & manage risk
	- Conservative approach, proven techniques, distanced from change
	- Caution is the principal focus
- Risk neutral
	- Dispassionate tolerance & ability to accept & manage risk
	- No defined philosophies, reaction oriented, measured indifference
	- Balance is the principal focus
- Risk taking
	- High tolerance & ability to accept and manage risk
	- Challenges current thinking, innovative approach
	- Experimentation is the principal focus
# WS
1. How will a risk-management plan change the endemic culture in both the business and the project?
	- Becoming proactive from a reactive risk management style, everyone involved improves collaboration and more accountability for the risks
2. What are 'real' examples of both negative and positive risks in George's project?
	- Employees might not like new software, but the new software might allow for more productivity/innovation, time consuming, cybersecurity and collaboration, reduction of legal issues
3. How would you scale and prioritise these risks to enable a targeted response?
	- Using a risk matrix, assigning risk ratings and rank them, important ones first - SWOT technique, subject matter experts
4. What existing controls and 'new' treatment strategies could George expect to see in the risk register?
	- Updating risk matrix to current situation, updating processes, including positive risks to take advantage of
5. Why is the notion of shared responsibility for risk so important to George?
	- If everyone feels like they have a responsibility to contribute they will more likely stick to it, different people are aware of different parts allowing for identifying more different risks, hold different people accountable for different risks
6. Should George feel completely at ease once risk treatment has been assigned?
	- The controls might change, the risks might change, the project will evolve over time, what risks are no longer relevant, new controls? new risks?
