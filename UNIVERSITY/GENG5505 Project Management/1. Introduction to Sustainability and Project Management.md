## Project and Project Management
- **Project**: a temporary endeavour undertaken to create a unique product, service or result
	- A collection of activities and tasks designed to achieve a specific but temporary goal of the organisation, with specific performance or quality requirements, all the while subject to time and cost constraints
- **Project Management**: the application of knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities to meet project requirements. Project management is accomplished through the application and integration of the project management processes of initiating, planning, executing, monitoring and controlling and closing
	- Initiating
	- Planning
	- Executing, Monitoring and Controlling
	- Closing
	- **Key Management Principles**: planning, organising, leading and controlling
	- A scheduled solution to a problem, opportunity or business needs
- The **project manager** is responsible and continually under pressure to accomplish the best possible project results

## Background for a Sustainable Project Management Approach
- **Sustainability**: Meeting the needs of people today without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs
- A company's ability to achieve its business goals and increase long-term shareholder value by integrating economic, environmental and social opportunities into its business strategies = Triple Bottom Line (TBL) approach
	- **TBL** = Social, Environment and Economic
- The sustainable enterprise is an organic, mutually emergent system that is connected economically, environmentally and socially to the world
- The long term financial interests of a company are not mutually exclusive with acting fairly in the interests of stakeholders
- **Possible Indicators for Measuring Sustainability**
	- Economic sustainability
	- Environmental sustainability
	- Social sustainability

# Project Management Life Cycle
### 1. Concept Stage / Initiation
- Idea stage where project is conceived
- Preliminary goals, deliverables and strategic vision alignment, problems raised, impact assessment with TBL and life cycle, potential benefits identified, alternative approaches researched and provisional costing determined
### 2. Planning Stage / Schedule
- All work required is planned and scheduled
- Objectives finalised, resources assigned, quality signed off, final costing, timing agreed, admin matters are determined
### 3. Execution Stage / Monitoring and Controlling
- Tracking actual progress using schedule
- All work is monitored, controlled and corrected
- Schedules reviewed, revised and updated
### 4. Finalisation / Completion, Termination and Handover
- Deliverable handed over to client
- Resources disposed or reassigned
- Project evaluated, report written, presented, admin closed

# Identifying Project Variables and Constraints
- Time: Provisional timeframe including start, finish & deadline dates
- Cost: Provisional budget & associated funding requirement & approvals
- Scope: Unambiguous & specific - i.e. deliverables, performance, quality standards, TBL …). Initial description of output as to enable measurement
- Resources: Nominated assignment &commitment of people, materials, equipment, & finance (Unambiguous quality specifications & performance standards to be identified & agreed upon among stakeholders).
- TBL and life cycle thinking steps

# Common Project Failure Causes

- Delivered over time (or even under)
- Client refusal to accept
- Lack of end-user involvement
- Not supported by key stakeholders
- Poorly understood scope
- Too many scope variations
- Inability to identify, prioritise and manage organisational wide projects
- Poorly lead and managed
- Lack of executive management mandate
- Lack of senior management support
- Lack of project management maturity
- Lack of coordinated approach
- Lack of formal methodology
- Lack of sufficient time, funding and skilled resources
- Inability to measure, report and adjust performance
- Lack of communicated, visible and demonstrated authority
- Functional conflicts
- Lack of transparent accountability
- Resistance to modify estimates
- Over-reliance and poor management of external parties
- Lack of momentum
- Reliance on reactive and remedial action
- Unauthorised scope variation
- Lack of authority to stop the project
- Lack of commitment to plan
- No contingency
- Lack of organisational capacity
- Poor management skills
- Poorly defined roles and responsibilities
- Not in line with TBL and LCT
