# Structure of Induction Motor
- Also called asynchronous motor
	- 3 phase and single phase
	- 90% of industrial motors are induction motors
	- Rarely used as a generator
- Why is it called an induction motor
	- The electric current in the rotor needed to produce torque is obtained by electromagnetic induction from the magnetic field of the stator winding
## Types of Rotor
### Cage Rotor
- Consists of a series of conducting aluminium bars laid into slots carved in the face of the rotor and shorted at either end by large shorting rings
### Wound Rotor
- A wound rotor has a complete set of three-phase windings that are similar to the windings on the stator. The three phases of the rotor windings are usually wye connected and the ends of the three rotor windings are tied to slip rings on the rotor's shaft

## Induction Machine - Transformer Action
- The rotor voltage which produces the rotor current and the rotor magnetic field is induced in rotor winding

## Induction Machine - Slip
- The rotor speed differs from the synchronous speed of the RMF
- The difference between the two is called Slip
- In order to keep rotating (motor) $N_{r}< N_{s}$ $$s=\frac{slip\_speed}{sync\_speed}=\frac{N_{slip}}{N_{s}}=\frac{\omega_{s}-\omega_{r}}{\omega_{s}}$$
