### Ricardo Roriz, Jorge Cabral, Tiago Gomes

`R. Roriz, J. Cabral, and T. Gomes, “Automotive LiDAR Technology: A Survey,” _IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems_, vol. 23, no. 7, pp. 1–16, 2021, doi: https://doi.org/10.1109/tits.2021.3086804.`

*Abstract— Nowadays, and more than a decade after the first
steps towards autonomous driving, we keep heading to achieve
fully autonomous vehicles on our roads, with LiDAR sensors
being a key instrument for the success of this technology.
Such advances trigger the emergence of new players in the
automotive industry, and along with car manufacturers, this
sector represents a multibillion-dollar market where everyone
wants to take a share. To understand recent advances and
technologies behind LiDAR, this article presents a survey on
LiDAR sensors for the automotive industry. With this work,
we show the measurement principles and imaging techniques
currently being used, going through a review of commercial
systems and development solutions available in the market today.
Furthermore, we highlight the current and future challenges,
providing insights on how both research and industry can step
towards better LiDAR solutions.*

## LiDAR Setup
- Contains:
	- a transmitter: emits light between 250 to 1600 nanometres
	- a receiver: gathers, analyses and computes the reflected signal. Contains:
		- a telescope to collect photons
		- an optical analyser to filter wavelengths, polarisation states
		- a data acquisition module, calculates elapsed pulse time and stores information

## Basic Concepts
$$R = 0.5c\tau$$
- $R$ is the range
- $c$ is the speed of light
- $\tau$ is the round trip delay
#### Time of Flight (ToF)
- Is the round trip delay
- Is obtained by modulating the frequency, phase, intensity, etc. of the transmitted light and measuring the time for that modulation pattern to be detected at the receiver

#### Important Performance Metrics
1. Detection Range
2. Transmitted Power
	1. Limited by eye safety regulation
3. Wavelength
	1. 905nm and 1550nm, allowing possible light spatial resolution to order of 0.1degrees
4. Field of View
5. Precision, Accuracy and Resolution
6. Pulse, Scan and Frame Rate

## LiDAR Measurement
1. Pulsed ToF
2. Amplitude Modulated Continuous Wave (AMCW)
	1. Unlike ToF, not sharp pulses, uses modulating intensity
3. Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW)

## LiDAR Imaging Systems
1. Rotor-based mechanical LiDAR
	1. Mechanical rotation system that spins the scanning part
	2. Vertically the FoV is defined by the number of existing emitter/receptor pairs, also known as channels
2. Scanning Solid State LiDAR
3. Full Solid State LiDAR
4. Emerging Technologies and Hybrid LiDAR Systems
	1. Liquid-Immersed Polygon LiDAR
	2. VCSEL
	3. Spectrum-Scan
	4. Micro-Motion Technology
	5. Hybrid Solutions
